Assault & Violent Crimes

Assault 3rd Degree (PL § 120.00)

Assault in the third degree (Penal Law § 120.00) is a New York crime that accuses someone of intentionally or recklessly causing physical injury to another person, which the law defines as any impairment or substantial pain, and it is the most common assault charge in New York City. It may mean New York prosecutors believe a punch, a shove, or a scuffle in a bar, on the street, at a game, or on the subway caused someone pain or a visible injury, and it can carry up to 364 days in jail, three years of probation, and a criminal record that shows up on background checks. Forbes Stranger defends students, professionals, workers, and individuals in Assault 3rd degree prosecutions in every borough’s criminal court. When one moment in a crowd becomes an assault case, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Assault 2nd Degree (PL § 120.05)

Assault in the second degree (Penal Law § 120.05) is a New York violent felony that accuses someone of causing serious physical injury with the statutorily required mental state, intentionally causing physical injury using a deadly weapon or dangerous instrument, or satisfying one of the separate protected-person theories; the age-65 theory also requires that the actor be more than ten years younger than the victim. It may mean New York prosecutors believe a fight outside a bar, a confrontation on the street or subway, or an object thrown in anger caused an injury serious enough to make it a felony, and it can carry up to seven years in state prison, post-release supervision, and a violent felony record that can raise the sentence on any future case. Forbes Stranger defends students, professionals, workers, and individuals in Assault 2nd degree prosecutions in every borough. When a single fight is charged as a violent felony, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Assault 1st Degree (PL § 120.10)

Assault in the first degree (Penal Law § 120.10) is one of New York’s most serious violent felonies, and it accuses someone of intentionally causing serious physical injury with a deadly weapon or dangerous instrument, intentionally causing serious and permanent disfigurement, causing serious injury through conduct showing depraved indifference to human life, or causing serious injury during another felony such as a robbery or burglary. It may mean New York prosecutors believe a stabbing, slashing, shooting, or beating left someone with lasting harm, and it can carry a mandatory minimum of five years and up to 25 years in state prison, often charged alongside attempted murder or weapons charges. Forbes Stranger defends individuals, young adults, and professionals in Assault 1st degree prosecutions in New York State Supreme Court in every borough. When prosecutors are seeking decades in prison, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Gang Assault Charges (PL §§ 120.06, 120.07)

Gang assault (Penal Law §§ 120.06, 120.07) is a New York violent felony that accuses someone of causing serious physical injury to another person while aided by two or more other people who were actually present, and despite its name, it requires no gang membership, but does require the statutory intent and injury elements and aid from at least two others actually present. It may mean New York prosecutors believe you were part of a group fight outside a club, a pile-on on the street, or a brawl captured on cell phone video, even if you never threw the punch that caused the injury, and it can carry three and a half to 15 years in state prison for gang assault in the second degree and five to 25 years for the first degree. Forbes Stranger defends students, young adults, and individuals in gang assault prosecutions in every borough. When being in the wrong group at the wrong moment is charged as a violent felony, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Assault on a Police Officer (PL §§ 120.05(3), 120.11)

Assault on a police officer is a New York violent felony that accuses someone of causing physical injury to a police officer, peace officer, or other first responder while trying to prevent them from doing their job (Penal Law § 120.05(3)). The charge becomes more serious when the officer suffers serious injury (Penal Law § 120.08) or is seriously injured with a weapon (Penal Law § 120.11). It may mean New York prosecutors believe you injured an officer during a stop, an arrest, a protest, or a struggle, even if the injury was minor and happened as you pulled away or fell, and it can carry up to seven years in state prison for Assault 2nd and up to 25 years, with a five-year mandatory minimum, for aggravated assault on a police officer. Forbes Stranger defends students, protesters, workers, and individuals charged with assaulting police officers in every borough. When the police are your accusers, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Resisting Arrest and Obstruction Charges (PL §§ 205.30, 195.05)

Resisting arrest (Penal Law § 205.30) is a New York crime that accuses someone of trying to prevent a police officer from making an authorized arrest, and obstructing governmental administration (Penal Law § 195.05) accuses someone of using force, intimidation, or physical interference to block police or other officials from doing their jobs. It may mean New York prosecutors believe you pulled away, tensed up, or refused to give your hands during an arrest, or stepped in while police were arresting someone else, and it can carry up to 364 days in jail on each charge, often added on top of whatever the arrest was originally for. Forbes Stranger defends students, protesters, workers, and individuals in resisting arrest and obstruction prosecutions in every borough’s criminal court, starting with whether the arrest was lawful in the first place. When a police encounter turns into extra charges, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Assaulting an MTA Worker, Nurse, Teacher, or Store Employee (PL § 120.05)

Assaults on certain transit, healthcare, school, and other protected workers may qualify as second-degree assault under specified subdivisions of Penal Law § 120.05. Assault on a retail worker is a distinct class E felony under Penal Law § 120.19 and requires intent to prevent the retail worker from performing an act within the scope of employment, physical injury, and knowledge or reason to know of the worker’s status. Protected workers include subway and bus operators, conductors, station agents, nurses and hospital workers, teachers and school staff, sanitation workers, and traffic agents. It may mean New York prosecutors believe a shove, a punch, or a struggle during an argument over a fare, a hospital wait, a school dispute, or a store’s theft policy caused even minor pain, and the applicable prison exposure and violent-felony classification depend on the particular offense: second-degree assault is a class D violent felony, whereas assault on a retail worker under § 120.19 is a class E felony. Forbes Stranger defends commuters, patients, parents, shoppers, and individuals charged with assaulting protected workers in every borough. When a single moment of frustration is charged as a violent felony, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Arrested After a Bar Fight or Street Fight

An arrest after a bar fight or street fight usually brings assault charges under Penal Law Article 120, ranging from misdemeanor assault in the third degree to felony assault in the second degree if a bottle, glass, or other object was involved or someone was seriously hurt. It may mean New York prosecutors are relying on the account of whoever called the police or stayed at the scene, and it can lead to a criminal record, jail time, and a felony charge even if you were defending yourself or a friend or did not start the fight. Forbes Stranger defends students, professionals, bouncers, bar staff, and individuals arrested after fights at bars, clubs, restaurants, and on the street in every borough. When a night out ends in handcuffs, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Arrested After a Fight on the Subway

An arrest after a fight on the subway usually brings assault charges under Penal Law Article 120, and the charge can jump to a violent felony (Penal Law § 120.05) if the other person was a transit worker, was 65 or older, or was hurt with an object. It may mean New York prosecutors believe a dispute over a seat, a push on a crowded platform, or a confrontation in a train car caused an injury, often based on a viral video or a single witness, and it can lead to a criminal record, jail time, and up to seven years in state prison when charged as a felony. Forbes Stranger defends commuters, students, workers, and individuals arrested after fights on the subway, on buses, and in transit stations in every borough. When a commute ends in an assault charge, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Road Rage Arrests

A road rage arrest brings criminal charges over a confrontation between drivers, cyclists, or pedestrians, most often assault (Penal Law Article 120), menacing (Penal Law §§ 120.13-120.15), reckless endangerment (Penal Law §§ 120.20, 120.25), or criminal mischief. A car itself can be treated as a dangerous instrument, which turns an assault into a felony. It may mean New York prosecutors believe you cut someone off, followed them, got out of your car, struck their vehicle, or swerved at them during an argument on the road, and it can lead to felony charges carrying up to seven years in state prison, the suspension of your license, and the loss of a job that depends on driving. Forbes Stranger defends drivers, rideshare and delivery drivers, cyclists, commercial drivers, and individuals in road rage prosecutions in every borough. When a moment in traffic becomes a criminal case, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Stabbing and Slashing Charges

A stabbing or slashing arrest usually brings felony assault charges: assault in the second degree (Penal Law § 120.05) when the applicable intent, injury, and weapon elements are established, and assault in the first degree (Penal Law § 120.10) when prosecutors can prove a distinct qualifying theory such as intentional serious physical injury with a deadly weapon or dangerous instrument, often with weapons possession or attempted murder charges added. It may mean New York prosecutors believe a knife, box cutter, razor, or broken bottle was used in a fight, a robbery, or a confrontation on the street or subway, and it can carry up to 25 years in state prison, with a five-year mandatory minimum for Assault 1st. Forbes Stranger defends young adults, workers, and individuals in stabbing and slashing prosecutions in every borough, including cases where the knife was used in self-defense. When prosecutors say you used a blade, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Menacing and Brandishing a Weapon (PL §§ 120.13-120.15)

Menacing (Penal Law §§ 120.13-120.15) is a New York crime that accuses someone of intentionally placing another person in fear of physical injury or death, most often by displaying a knife, a bat, a gun, or even something that looks like a gun. Menacing a police officer with a weapon is a separate violent felony (Penal Law § 120.18). It may mean New York prosecutors believe you pulled out a weapon or made a threatening move during an argument on the street, at work, in traffic, or in a store, even though no one was touched, and it can carry up to 364 days in jail for menacing in the second degree, up to four years in state prison for menacing in the first degree, and up to seven years for menacing a police officer. Forbes Stranger defends workers, business owners, licensed gun owners, and individuals in menacing prosecutions in every borough’s criminal court. When showing a weapon is charged as a crime of violence, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Reckless Endangerment Charges (PL §§ 120.20, 120.25)

Reckless endangerment (Penal Law §§ 120.20, 120.25) is a New York crime that accuses someone of recklessly engaging in conduct that creates a substantial risk of serious physical injury to another person, or, in the first degree, a grave risk of death under circumstances showing depraved indifference to human life, even if no one is actually hurt. It may mean New York prosecutors believe firing a gun into the air, throwing something from a roof or window, driving into a crowd or onto a sidewalk, or another dangerous act put people at risk, and it can carry up to 364 days in jail for reckless endangerment in the second degree and up to seven years in state prison for the first degree. Forbes Stranger defends drivers, young adults, workers, and individuals in reckless endangerment prosecutions in every borough. When prosecutors charge you for what could have happened, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Arrested for Making Threats (Terroristic Threat, PL § 490.20)

Threat charges in New York range from making a terroristic threat (Penal Law § 490.20), a violent felony that accuses someone of threatening a violent crime to intimidate the public or influence the government, to making a threat of mass harm (Penal Law § 240.78) and falsely reporting an incident (Penal Law §§ 240.50-240.60). These cover threats of shootings, bombings, or attacks on schools, workplaces, and public places. It may mean New York prosecutors believe a post, comment, text, video, or statement made in anger, as a joke, or online was a real threat, and it can carry up to seven years in state prison, school expulsion or job loss, and federal charges if the threat crossed state lines (18 U.S.C. § 875). Forbes Stranger defends students, employees, gamers, social media users, and individuals in threat prosecutions in every borough. When words online are treated as a crime, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Harassment 2nd Degree (PL § 240.26)

Harassment in the second degree (Penal Law § 240.26) is a New York offense that accuses someone of shoving, striking, kicking, or otherwise making physical contact with another person, or threatening to, with intent to harass, annoy, or alarm them, or of following someone in public or engaging in conduct that seriously annoys them. It may mean New York prosecutors believe a push on the street, a confrontation with a neighbor, coworker, or stranger, or a heated moment in a store or on the subway was meant to harass, and while it is a violation rather than a crime, it can carry up to 15 days in jail, an order of protection, and an arrest record that follows you until the case is resolved and sealed. Forbes Stranger defends neighbors, coworkers, commuters, students, and individuals in harassment 2nd degree cases in every borough’s criminal court, including cases where a harassment plea is offered to resolve a more serious assault charge. When a minor confrontation becomes a court case, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Disorderly Conduct (PL § 240.20)

Disorderly conduct (Penal Law § 240.20) is a New York offense that accuses someone of fighting, threatening behavior, unreasonable noise, abusive language in public, blocking traffic or a sidewalk, or refusing to leave when police order a crowd to disperse, with intent to cause, or recklessly risking, public inconvenience, annoyance, or alarm. It may mean police arrested or ticketed you after an argument outside a bar, a confrontation on the street, a protest, or a crowd gathering, and while it is a violation rather than a crime, it can carry up to 15 days in jail, fines, and a court date that brings a warrant if you miss it. Forbes Stranger defends students, protesters, workers, and individuals charged with disorderly conduct in every borough’s criminal court, and negotiates disorderly conduct resolutions that keep more serious charges off your record. When an arrest for a public disturbance threatens your record, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Hate Crime Charges (PL § 485.05)

A hate crime (Penal Law § 485.05) is a New York charge that accuses someone of committing an offense, such as assault, harassment, menacing, or property damage, because of the victim’s actual or perceived race, religion, national origin, gender, gender identity, sexual orientation, age, or disability. A hate crime designation raises the charge by one full level, turning many misdemeanors into felonies (Penal Law § 485.10). It may mean New York prosecutors and the NYPD Hate Crime Task Force believe words spoken in an argument, a social media post, or symbols drawn during an incident prove the motive, and it can bring years more in state prison, public identification as a hate crime defendant, and lasting damage to your career and reputation. Forbes Stranger defends students, professionals, protesters, and individuals in hate crime prosecutions in every borough. When prosecutors claim bias turned an incident into a hate crime, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Kidnapping Charges (PL §§ 135.20, 135.25)

Kidnapping (Penal Law §§ 135.20, 135.25) is one of New York’s most serious violent felonies, and it accuses someone of abducting another person, meaning restraining them with intent to hide them where they are unlikely to be found, or by using or threatening deadly force. Kidnapping in the first degree is charged when prosecutors claim ransom, a restraint of more than 12 hours for a violent purpose, or a death. It may mean New York prosecutors believe a dispute over money or drugs, a robbery, or a custody fight involved holding someone against their will, and it can carry five to 25 years in state prison for kidnapping in the second degree and up to life for the first degree, while taking your own child in violation of a custody order is charged separately as custodial interference (Penal Law §§ 135.45, 135.50). Forbes Stranger defends individuals, parents, and young adults in kidnapping and custodial interference prosecutions in every borough. When prosecutors are seeking a life sentence, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Arson Charges (PL §§ 150.05-150.20)

Arson (Penal Law §§ 150.05-150.20) is a New York crime that accuses someone of intentionally or recklessly damaging a building or vehicle by starting a fire or causing an explosion. The charge rises from a felony in the fourth degree to a violent felony in the second degree when a nonparticipant was present and the defendant knew or reasonably could have anticipated that presence, and to arson in the first degree when someone is seriously injured or an explosive or incendiary device was used. It may mean FDNY fire marshals and New York prosecutors believe a fire in an apartment, a car, a business, or a public place was set on purpose, sometimes to collect insurance, and it can carry five to 25 years in state prison for arson in the second degree and up to life for the first degree. Forbes Stranger defends homeowners, tenants, business owners, protesters, and individuals in arson prosecutions in every borough. When prosecutors claim an accidental fire was a crime, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Arrested at a Protest (Riot and Unlawful Assembly, PL §§ 240.05-240.10)

A protest arrest in New York can bring charges ranging from disorderly conduct and unlawful assembly (Penal Law § 240.10) to riot (Penal Law §§ 240.05, 240.06) and incitement to riot (Penal Law § 240.08). Trespass, criminal mischief, obstructing governmental administration, resisting arrest, and assault on a police officer are often added, and charges are frequently filed against dozens of people at once. It may mean police arrested you at a march, a rally, a campus encampment, or a sit-in based on where you were standing rather than anything you did, and it can lead to a criminal record, felony charges carrying up to four years in state prison for first-degree riot, school discipline, and, for international students and other non-citizens, visa and immigration consequences. Forbes Stranger defends students, activists, journalists, legal observers, and individuals arrested at protests in every borough. When exercising your rights ends in handcuffs, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Charged With Assault but It Was Self-Defense (PL § 35.15)

Self-defense, called justification under New York law (Penal Law § 35.15), allows you to use the physical force you reasonably believe is necessary to protect yourself or someone else from an imminent attack. Deadly force is allowed when you reasonably believe the other person is using or about to use deadly force, though outside your home you generally must retreat first if you can do so in complete safety. It may mean police arrested you because you were the one still standing, the one with the weapon, or the one accused first, and it can lead to felony assault charges unless your lawyer builds the self-defense case early, because once it is raised, prosecutors must disprove it beyond a reasonable doubt. Forbes Stranger defends individuals, workers, business owners, and bystanders who used force to protect themselves or others, in every borough. When defending yourself gets you arrested, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Teen Arrested for Assault or a Fight at School (CPL Article 722)

When a teenager is arrested for assault or a fight at school in New York, Raise the Age (Criminal Procedure Law Article 722) sends most 16- and 17-year-olds charged with felonies to the Youth Part of Supreme Court as adolescent offenders, while misdemeanors and cases involving younger children usually go to Family Court. It may mean school safety agents or officers arrested your child after a fight in a hallway, on a school bus, or after school, and it can lead to felony assault charges, detention, a superintendent’s suspension hearing (Education Law § 3214), and a record that follows a young person into college admissions and jobs. Forbes Stranger defends teenagers and young adults in assault cases and school disciplinary hearings in the Youth Part, Family Court, and criminal court in every borough. When your child’s future is on the line, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between them and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Bail and Release After an Assault Arrest (CPL § 510.10)

Bail and release after an assault arrest are decided at arraignment under New York’s bail law (Criminal Procedure Law § 510.10). The law requires release for most misdemeanor assaults but allows judges to set bail, impose supervised release, or order detention for violent felonies such as Assault 2nd and Assault 1st. It may mean prosecutors will ask for high bail or remand based on the injury, a weapon, your record, or past missed court dates, and it can mean weeks or months in jail before your case is decided if the arguments for release are not made forcefully at the first appearance. Forbes Stranger represents individuals and families at arraignment and bail hearings in every borough. When freedom before trial is on the line, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Second Violent Felony Offender Charges (PL §§ 70.04, 70.08)

A second violent felony offender (Penal Law § 70.04) is someone convicted of a violent felony, such as Assault 2nd or Robbery 2nd, after a prior violent felony conviction within the last ten years, not counting time spent in prison. Two or more prior violent felonies can make you a persistent violent felony offender (Penal Law § 70.08). It may mean prosecutors have filed a predicate felony statement (Criminal Procedure Law § 400.15) to use your record against you, and it can bring a mandatory minimum of five years for an Assault 2nd conviction, ten years for Assault 1st, and, for a persistent violent felony offender, a sentence of up to life in state prison. Forbes Stranger defends individuals with prior violent felony convictions in every borough. When your past is being used to take the rest of your life, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.
