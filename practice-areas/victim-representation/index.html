Do I Need a Lawyer If I’m the Victim of a Crime?

You do not need a lawyer to report a crime, but in a New York criminal case the prosecutor represents the People of the State of New York, not you, so no one in the courtroom has to protect your interests unless you have your own lawyer. What Happens After You Report a Crime in NYC

After you report a crime in New York City, the NYPD writes up a complaint report and may assign a detective to investigate. If someone is arrested, an assistant district attorney will usually interview you and ask you to sign a sworn statement so the case can go forward, and the person arrested is usually arraigned within about 24 hours, where a judge decides on release and can issue a temporary order of protection (CPL §§ 530.12, 530.13). Felony cases then go to a grand jury, where you may be asked to testify, before moving on to hearings, a plea, or a trial. Victim Advocate or Lawyer: What’s the Difference?

A victim advocate offers support, goes to court with you, and helps with services and compensation forms, but an advocate who works for the district attorney’s office or the NYPD is part of the prosecution team, and what you tell them can end up in the discovery the defense receives (CPL § 245.20(1)(e)). A lawyer represents only you, gives you legal advice protected by attorney-client privilege, and can act for you in court and with the prosecutor, which an advocate cannot do. The defense may use notes from your conversations with an advocate to attack your account, and police and prosecutors may make decisions about your case that an advocate has no power to challenge, and without a lawyer you can lose control over your case and your private information. Crime Victims’ Rights in New York (Executive Law § 641)

Crime victims’ rights in New York are set out in the state’s fair treatment standards. How to Get Restitution Paid (PL § 60.27)

Can I Sue the Person Who Harmed Me? (CPLR § 213-b)
