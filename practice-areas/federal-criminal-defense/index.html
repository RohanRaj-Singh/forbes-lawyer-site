Federal Criminal Defense

Southern and Eastern Districts of New York.

Representation in federal investigations and prosecutions — from the first contact with the government through resolution or trial.

Federal cases run on a different clock. The investigation usually begins long before an indictment, and the decisions made in that interval — what is said to an agent, what is produced under a subpoena, what is agreed to in a proffer — tend to define the case that follows. The firm is built to be engaged at that point, not after it.

Before indictment

Grand jury subpoenas, target letters, search warrants, and requests for interview all present choices that are difficult to undo. The firm advises clients through the investigative stage with the assumption that anything said may later be used, and structures engagement around protecting the record.

The federal process

From initial appearance and detention proceedings through discovery, motions, plea negotiations, and sentencing, each stage has its own conventions. Federal discovery is often voluminous; it is reviewed as evidence rather than as paperwork. Where trial is the right course, the case is tried.

Investigations spanning agencies

The firm’s work has involved investigations touching the United States Attorney’s Office, the New York State Attorney General, Homeland Security, and other federal law-enforcement agencies. Matters that cross jurisdictions require one coherent defense strategy rather than several disconnected responses.

Parallel exposure

Federal exposure rarely arrives alone. Regulatory, civil, employment, and reputational consequences often run alongside the criminal matter. Strategy accounts for them from the outset, because a resolution in one forum can constrain the others.
