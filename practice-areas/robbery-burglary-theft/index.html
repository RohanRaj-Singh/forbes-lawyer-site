Robbery, Burglary & Theft Crimes

Robbery 3rd Degree (PL § 160.05)

Robbery in the third degree (Penal Law § 160.05) is a New York felony that accuses someone of forcibly stealing property, meaning using or threatening physical force to take something or to keep it after taking it, even without a weapon or an injury. It may mean New York prosecutors believe a shove, a grab, or a threat during a theft, a dispute over money, or an escape from a store turned a larceny into a robbery, and it can carry up to seven years in state prison and a felony record. Forbes Stranger defends young adults, workers, students, and individuals in Robbery 3rd degree prosecutions in every borough. When a theft is charged as a robbery, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Robbery 2nd Degree (PL § 160.10)

Robbery in the second degree (Penal Law § 160.10) is a New York violent felony that accuses someone of forcibly stealing property while aided by another person who was actually present, while causing physical injury, while displaying what appears to be a gun, or by taking a car. It may mean New York prosecutors believe you were part of a group that took a phone, money, or a bag, that someone was hurt during a theft, or that an object in your hand or pocket looked like a weapon, and it carries a mandatory minimum of three and a half years and up to 15 years in state prison. Forbes Stranger defends young adults, students, and individuals in Robbery 2nd degree prosecutions in every borough. When being with the wrong people is charged as a violent felony, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Robbery 1st Degree: Armed Robbery (PL § 160.15)

Robbery in the first degree (Penal Law § 160.15), often called armed robbery, is a New York violent felony that accuses someone of forcibly stealing property while armed with a deadly weapon, using or threatening to use a dangerous instrument such as a knife, displaying what appears to be a gun, or causing serious physical injury. It may mean New York prosecutors believe a gun, knife, or other weapon was shown or used during a street robbery, a store robbery, or a home invasion, and it carries a mandatory minimum of five years and up to 25 years in state prison, though proving the gun was not loaded or operable can reduce the charge (Penal Law § 160.15(4)). Forbes Stranger defends young adults and individuals in Robbery 1st degree and armed robbery prosecutions in New York State Supreme Court in every borough. When prosecutors say you used a weapon, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Carjacking Charges (PL §§ 160.10, 160.15)

Carjacking allegations in New York generally involve robbery charges when prosecutors accuse someone of taking a vehicle by force or threats (Penal Law §§ 160.10, 160.15). Depending on the allegations, charges may involve weapons, injuries, multiple participants, or related vehicle offenses and can carry substantial prison exposure. Charged With Robbery for Shoplifting (PL § 160.05)

Being charged with robbery for shoplifting happens because New York law defines robbery to include using force to keep property right after taking it (Penal Law § 160.00). A shoplifting stop that ends with a push, a pull, or a struggle with a security guard can be charged as robbery in the third degree (Penal Law § 160.05), or robbery in the second degree if someone was with you or the guard was hurt. It may mean New York prosecutors are treating a petit larceny over a few items as a felony because of what happened at the door, and it can carry up to seven years in state prison for Robbery 3rd and up to 15 years for Robbery 2nd, with assault charges possible because retail workers are now covered by New York’s felony assault law. Forbes Stranger defends students, young adults, workers, and individuals charged with robbery after shoplifting stops in every borough. Burglary 3rd Degree (PL § 140.20)

Burglary in the third degree (Penal Law § 140.20) is a New York felony that accuses someone of entering or remaining unlawfully in a building, such as a store, office, warehouse, garage, construction site, or basement, with intent to commit a crime inside, and that crime can be any crime, not just theft. It may mean New York prosecutors believe you went into a closed business, a restricted area, or a store you had been banned from with intent to steal, and it can carry up to seven years in state prison and a felony record, even if nothing was taken. Forbes Stranger defends young adults, workers, and individuals in Burglary 3rd degree prosecutions in every borough. Burglary 2nd Degree (PL § 140.25)

Burglary in the second degree (Penal Law § 140.25) is a New York violent felony that accuses someone of entering or remaining unlawfully in a dwelling, such as a house, apartment, or hotel room, with intent to commit a crime inside, or of committing any burglary while armed, while causing injury, or while displaying what appears to be a gun. It may mean New York prosecutors believe you went into a home or apartment, even one belonging to an ex, a relative, or a former roommate, without permission and with intent to take something or harm someone, and it carries a mandatory minimum of three and a half years and up to 15 years in state prison. Burglary 1st Degree and Home Invasion Charges (PL § 140.30)

Burglary in the first degree (Penal Law § 140.30) is New York’s most serious burglary charge, and it accuses someone of unlawfully entering a home with intent to commit a crime while armed with a weapon, displaying what appears to be a gun, using or threatening to use a dangerous instrument, or injuring someone inside. This is how New York prosecutes what most people call a home invasion. It may mean New York prosecutors believe you entered a house or apartment with others, a weapon, or a plan to rob the people inside, often charged alongside Robbery 1st, and it carries a mandatory minimum of five years and up to 25 years in state prison, though proving a displayed gun was not loaded or operable can reduce the charge. Criminal Trespass 3rd Degree (PL § 140.10)

Criminal trespass in the third degree (Penal Law § 140.10) is a New York misdemeanor that accuses someone of knowingly entering or remaining unlawfully on property that is fenced or enclosed to keep people out, on school grounds in violation of posted rules, in a public housing building, or on railroad or subway tracks. It may mean police stopped you on a rooftop, in a closed park, at a construction site, in a schoolyard, or in a building lobby and decided you had no right to be there, and it can carry up to three months in jail and a criminal record that shows up on background checks. It may mean New York prosecutors believe you went into an ex’s apartment, stayed in a home after being told to leave, or occupied a vacant unit without permission, and it can carry up to 364 days in jail, an order of protection, and a criminal record, often as a lesser alternative charge in burglary cases. Possession of Burglar’s Tools (PL § 140.35)

Possession of burglar’s tools (Penal Law § 140.35) is a New York misdemeanor that accuses someone of carrying a tool or object commonly used to break into places, steal property, or avoid paying for services, under circumstances that suggest an intent to use it that way. Examples include screwdrivers, pry bars, lock picks, devices for removing security tags, and foil-lined “booster bags.” It may mean police found ordinary tools in your bag, car, or pockets during a shoplifting stop, a street stop, or a search near a building, and decided they were meant for a crime, and it can carry up to 364 days in jail and is often charged alongside petit larceny, burglary, or trespass. Petit Larceny (PL § 155.25)

Petit larceny (Penal Law § 155.25) is a New York misdemeanor that accuses someone of stealing property worth $1,000 or less, and it is the single most common theft charge in New York City, covering shoplifting, package theft, taking a phone, bike, or wallet, and theft from an employer. It may mean New York prosecutors believe you walked out of a store with unpaid items, kept property that was not yours, or took something from work, and it can carry up to 364 days in jail, three years of probation, and a theft conviction that employers, landlords, and immigration authorities treat as a crime of dishonesty. A civil demand letter is a demand for money after a shoplifting stop, sent by a store or a law firm working for it under New York’s retail civil recovery law (General Obligations Law § 11-105). The law lets merchants seek a civil penalty capped at $500 per incident, plus the value of any merchandise not recovered, and the letter can arrive whether or not you were arrested, charged, or convicted. It may mean the store is pursuing you separately from any criminal case, and it can lead to a small claims lawsuit if ignored, while paying it does not end a criminal case and anything you write back can be used against you in court. Organized Retail Theft Charges (PL §§ 155.30, 155.35, 460.20)

Organized retail theft is not a single New York charge. Prosecutors use the term for coordinated shoplifting by groups or repeat boosters, and they charge it as grand larceny (Penal Law §§ 155.30, 155.35), as criminal possession of stolen property against resellers, and in the largest cases as enterprise corruption (Penal Law § 460.20). Since 2024, New York law has let prosecutors add up thefts from different stores to reach felony levels, and it has created a new crime for fostering the sale of stolen goods. It may mean New York prosecutors believe you were part of a crew targeting drugstores, department stores, or luxury retailers, or that you bought or resold stolen merchandise online or out of a shop, and it can carry up to seven years in state prison for Grand Larceny 3rd and up to 25 years for enterprise corruption, often with conspiracy charges added. When a tag or a mural is charged as a crime, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Teen Arrested for Robbery (CPL Article 722)

When a teenager is arrested for robbery in New York, Raise the Age (Criminal Procedure Law Article 722) sends 16- and 17-year-olds charged with any degree of robbery (Penal Law §§ 160.05-160.15) to the Youth Part of Supreme Court as adolescent offenders. Children who are 14 or 15 can be prosecuted there as juvenile offenders for armed robbery or a robbery in which someone was hurt (Penal Law § 30.00). It may mean your son or daughter was with a group when another teen’s phone, sneakers, bike, or money was taken near a school, a subway station, or a park, and it can carry years in state prison and a violent felony record unless the case is moved to Family Court or ends in a sealed youthful offender adjudication (Criminal Procedure Law Article 720). Forbes Stranger defends teenagers and young adults charged with robbery in the Youth Part, Family Court, and Supreme Court in every borough. When being with the wrong group threatens your child’s future, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between them and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Theft Charges for Non-Citizens and Green Card Holders (8 U.S.C. § 1227)

Theft charges for non-citizens and green card holders carry consequences far beyond the criminal case. Federal immigration law treats most theft convictions, even petit larceny, as crimes involving moral turpitude, and it treats any theft or robbery conviction with a sentence of one year or more, even a suspended one, as an aggravated felony (8 U.S.C. §§ 1101(a)(43)(G), 1227(a)(2)). It may mean a guilty plea to shoplifting, larceny, stolen property, or robbery, even a plea to a reduced charge, could lead to deportation, mandatory immigration detention, denial of citizenship or a green card, or a bar on returning after travel abroad, and it can carry those consequences even when the criminal sentence is probation or no jail at all. Forbes Stranger defends green card holders, visa holders, students, workers, DACA recipients, and undocumented individuals charged with theft and robbery in every borough. When one theft charge could cost you the right to stay in this country, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.
