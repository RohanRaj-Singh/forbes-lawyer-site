Sex Crimes & SORA

Rape in the First Degree (PL § 130.35)

Rape in the first degree (Penal Law § 130.35) is a New York crime that accuses someone of vaginal, anal, or oral sexual contact by forcible compulsion, with a person who is physically helpless, or with a child under 11, or under 13 when the accused is 18 or older, including conduct once charged as criminal sexual act. It may mean New York prosecutors believe force was used or that the other person was unconscious or otherwise physically unable to communicate unwillingness, including where intoxication actually produced that condition, and it can carry up to 25 years in state prison, with a mandatory minimum of five years, plus post-release supervision and sex offender registration. These cases often turn on consent, intoxication, and the messages exchanged before and after, evidence that must be preserved immediately. Forbes Stranger defends students, professionals, executives, and individuals in first-degree rape prosecutions. When New York prosecutors charge you with the most serious degree of rape, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Rape in the Second Degree (PL § 130.30)

Rape in the second degree (Penal Law § 130.30) is a New York crime that covers specified sexual conduct by a person age 18 or older with someone under 15, and separately covers specified sexual conduct with someone incapable of consent due to mental disability or mental incapacitation without the same actor-age prerequisite, including conduct once charged as criminal sexual act. It may mean New York prosecutors believe the other person was too young to consent under the law, even if the relationship felt consensual, or was unable to consent because of a mental condition or incapacity, and it can carry up to seven years in state prison, plus post-release supervision and sex offender registration. Defenses such as the age-gap defense, available when the accused is less than four years older, must be raised from the start. Forbes Stranger defends young adults, students, professionals, and individuals in second-degree rape prosecutions. When a question of age or capacity turns a relationship into a felony, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Rape in the Third Degree (PL § 130.25)

Rape in the third degree (Penal Law § 130.25) is a New York crime that accuses someone of vaginal, anal, or oral sexual contact without the other person’s consent, even without force, or, for someone age 21 or older, with a person under 17, including conduct once charged as criminal sexual act. It may mean New York prosecutors believe the other person said no or clearly did not agree, or was under the age of consent, and it can carry up to four years in state prison, plus post-release supervision and sex offender registration. These cases often come down to one person’s word against another’s, so every message, witness, and timeline detail matters. Forbes Stranger defends students, young adults, professionals, and individuals in third-degree rape prosecutions. When a disagreement about consent becomes a felony charge, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Aggravated Sexual Abuse (PL §§ 130.65-a-130.70)

Aggravated sexual abuse (Penal Law §§ 130.65-a-130.70) is a New York crime that accuses someone of inserting a finger or an object into another person’s vagina, anus, or other intimate area without consent, by force, or with someone who is unable to consent because of age, incapacity, or physical helplessness. It may mean New York prosecutors believe sexual contact went beyond touching to penetration, often in an encounter involving alcohol, drugs, or a disputed timeline, and it can carry up to 25 years in state prison for the first degree, plus post-release supervision and sex offender registration. Medical evidence, DNA results, and the accuser’s own statements must be examined by experts before they are accepted as fact. Forbes Stranger defends students, professionals, medical providers, and individuals in aggravated sexual abuse prosecutions. When an accusation of penetration puts decades of your life on the line, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Sexual Abuse and Sexual Misconduct (PL §§ 130.20, 130.55-130.65)

Sexual abuse (Penal Law §§ 130.55-130.65) and sexual misconduct (Penal Law § 130.20) are New York crimes that accuse someone of touching another person’s sexual or intimate parts, even over clothing, or of sexual contact without consent, by force, or with someone unable to consent. It may mean New York prosecutors believe a touch at a party, at work, on public transit, or in a relationship was unwanted, and it can range from a misdemeanor carrying up to 364 days in jail to a first-degree felony carrying up to seven years in state prison, with possible sex offender registration. Even a misdemeanor sex conviction can require registration and follow you into every job, school, and housing application. Forbes Stranger defends students, professionals, workers, and individuals in sexual abuse and sexual misconduct prosecutions in every borough’s criminal court. When an encounter is recast as a sex crime, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Predatory Sexual Assault (PL §§ 130.95, 130.96)

Predatory sexual assault (Penal Law § 130.95) and predatory sexual assault against a child (Penal Law § 130.96) are New York crimes that accuse someone of first-degree rape or another first-degree sex offense combined with serious physical injury, a weapon, multiple victims, or a prior sex offense conviction, or of a first-degree sex offense against a child under 13. It may mean New York prosecutors believe the alleged conduct is among the most serious the Penal Law recognizes, and it can carry a minimum of 10 to 25 years and up to life in state prison, plus lifetime sex offender registration. Prosecutors often build these cases from multiple accusers or old allegations, each of which must be investigated and challenged on its own. Forbes Stranger defends individuals, professionals, and educators in predatory sexual assault prosecutions. When prosecutors are seeking a life sentence, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Forcible Touching and Subway Groping (PL §§ 130.52, 130.53)

Forcible touching (Penal Law § 130.52) is a New York crime that accuses someone of intentionally grabbing, squeezing, pinching, or rubbing against another person’s sexual or intimate parts for no legitimate purpose, and persistent sexual abuse is a felony only when the applicable qualifying prior-conviction requirements are met (Penal Law § 130.53). It may mean New York prosecutors believe a touch on a crowded subway car, a bus, in a bar, or on a sidewalk was intentional rather than accidental, and it can carry up to 364 days in jail, possible sex offender registration, and up to four years in state prison when charged as persistent sexual abuse. These cases often rest on a split-second identification in a crowd, with subway and station video that can be erased if it is not preserved quickly. Forbes Stranger defends commuters, students, professionals, and tourists in forcible touching and subway groping prosecutions in every borough’s criminal court. When a crowded train turns into a sex crime accusation, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Falsely Accused of Rape or Sexual Assault

Being falsely accused of rape or sexual assault means someone has told police, a school, an employer, or the public that you committed a sex offense under Penal Law Article 130 that you did not commit, often after a date, a party, a breakup, or a night involving alcohol. It may mean New York prosecutors are crediting the accuser’s account over yours, sometimes before hearing your side at all, and it can lead to arrest, an order of protection, felony charges carrying years in state prison, and sex offender registration if you are convicted. The texts, rideshare records, photos, and witnesses that show what really happened can disappear within days. Forbes Stranger defends students, professionals, executives, and individuals falsely accused of rape and sexual assault in every borough. When a false accusation threatens everything you have built, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Accused of a Sex Crime From Years Ago

An accusation of a sex crime from years ago is a report of alleged conduct that happened long in the past, and New York has eliminated the statute of limitations for the most serious sex offenses and extended it to 20 years for many others (Criminal Procedure Law § 30.10). It may mean New York prosecutors believe an allegation from a past relationship, a childhood, a college campus, or a workplace can still be charged, and it can lead to felony charges, prison time, sex offender registration, and civil lawsuits based on the same accusation. The passage of time cuts both ways, with lost evidence, faded memories, and changing stories that must be uncovered and used in your defense. Forbes Stranger defends professionals, executives, public figures, educators, and family members accused of sex crimes from years or decades ago. When the past is being used against you, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Accused of Child Sexual Abuse (Course of Sexual Conduct) (PL §§ 130.75, 130.80)

Course of sexual conduct against a child (Penal Law §§ 130.75, 130.80) is a New York crime that accuses someone of committing two or more acts of sexual conduct with a child under 11, or with a child under 13 when the accused is 18 or older, over a period of at least three months. It may mean New York prosecutors believe a child’s disclosure to a parent, teacher, doctor, or therapist describes repeated abuse, and it can carry up to 25 years in state prison for the first degree, plus post-release supervision and sex offender registration. These cases often depend on forensic interviews of children, which must be examined by experts for suggestive questioning and inconsistencies. Forbes Stranger defends parents, relatives, teachers, coaches, caregivers, and individuals accused of child sexual abuse in course of sexual conduct prosecutions. When an accusation this serious threatens your family and your freedom, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

New York State Child Pornography Charges (PL Article 263)

New York State child pornography charges (Penal Law Article 263) accuse someone of possessing, promoting, or producing a sexual performance by a child, most often charged as possessing a sexual performance by a child (Penal Law § 263.16) or promoting a sexual performance by a child (Penal Law § 263.15). It may mean New York prosecutors believe illegal images or videos were found on your phone, computer, or cloud accounts, and it can carry up to four years in state prison for possession and up to seven years for promoting, with each file potentially charged as a separate count and sex offender registration if you are convicted. Forensic questions about how files got onto a device, who used it, and whether they were ever opened can decide the case, along with whether it stays in state court or is referred for federal prosecution. Forbes Stranger defends individuals, professionals, students, and educators in New York State child pornography prosecutions. When a search of your devices puts your entire future at stake, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Online Sting Arrests and Luring Charges (PL § 235.22)

Online sting and luring charges accuse someone of using the internet, a dating app, or text messages to send sexual material to a minor and try to arrange a meeting, usually charged as disseminating indecent material to minors in the first degree (Penal Law § 235.22) or luring a child (Penal Law § 120.70). It may mean New York prosecutors believe chats with someone who said they were underage crossed the line, even when that person was actually an undercover officer, and it can carry up to seven years in state prison, along with attempted sex offense charges and sex offender registration. Entrapment, the full chat history, and how the conversation started must be examined before you say anything to investigators. Forbes Stranger defends individuals, professionals, students, and service members arrested in online sting operations in New York. When a sting operation is built to make an arrest, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Endangering the Welfare of a Child (EWOC) (PL § 260.10)

Endangering the welfare of a child (Penal Law § 260.10) is a New York class A misdemeanor that can involve allegations of conduct likely to endanger a child’s physical, mental, or moral welfare. The charge may arise alongside accusations involving domestic incidents, supervision, or sexual misconduct, and can carry jail time and consequences for employment or family relationships. Forbes Stranger represents parents, caregivers, professionals, and individuals accused of endangering a child throughout New York City. Revenge Porn and Deepfake Images (PL § 245.15)

Unlawful dissemination or publication of an intimate image (Penal Law § 245.15), commonly called revenge porn, is a New York crime that accuses someone of sharing or posting a nude or sexual image of another person without consent and with intent to cause harm. Since 2023, it also covers deepfakes and other digitally created or altered images. It may mean New York prosecutors believe a photo or video you sent, posted, or shared after a breakup was meant to humiliate or harm the person shown, and it can carry up to 364 days in jail under state or city law (NYC Administrative Code § 10-180), plus an order of protection, a court order to remove the images, and a civil lawsuit for damages (Civil Rights Law § 52-b). Forbes Stranger defends students, young adults, professionals, and individuals in revenge porn and deepfake image prosecutions in every borough’s criminal court. When a single post or message becomes a criminal case, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Hidden Cameras and Unlawful Surveillance (PL §§ 250.45-250.55)

Unlawful surveillance (Penal Law §§ 250.45, 250.50) is a New York crime that accuses someone of secretly recording or viewing another person undressing or engaged in sexual activity, or recording under their clothing, in a place where they had a reasonable expectation of privacy, such as a bathroom, bedroom, locker room, or short-term rental. Sharing those images is a separate crime (Penal Law §§ 250.55, 250.60). It may mean New York prosecutors believe a camera in your home, rental property, office, or phone captured someone without their knowledge, and it can carry up to four years in state prison for second-degree unlawful surveillance and up to seven years for the first degree, plus possible sex offender registration. Forbes Stranger defends landlords, Airbnb hosts, employers, professionals, students, and individuals in unlawful surveillance and hidden camera prosecutions. Public Lewdness and Indecent Exposure (PL §§ 245.00, 245.01)

Public lewdness (Penal Law § 245.00) is a New York crime that accuses someone of intentionally exposing their private or intimate parts in a lewd manner, or committing another lewd act, in a public place or where others can readily see. Exposure of a person (Penal Law § 245.01) covers lesser cases of public nudity. It may mean New York prosecutors believe something seen on a subway, in a park, through a window, or in a car was a deliberate sexual act rather than an accident or a misunderstanding, and it can carry up to 364 days in jail when charged as public lewdness in the first degree (Penal Law § 245.03), along with sex offender registration in some cases. Forbes Stranger defends commuters, students, professionals, and individuals in public lewdness and indecent exposure prosecutions in every borough’s criminal court. When a moment in public becomes a sex crime accusation, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Arrested for Solicitation (Patronizing a Prostitute) (PL §§ 230.04-230.06)

Patronizing a person for prostitution (Penal Law §§ 230.04-230.06), often called solicitation, is a New York crime that accuses someone of paying, agreeing to pay, or asking for sexual conduct in exchange for a fee, and it is often charged after NYPD sting operations using undercover officers or online ads. It may mean New York prosecutors believe a conversation on the street, in a car, at a hotel, or on a website or app was an offer to pay for sex, and it can carry up to 364 days in jail for the misdemeanor, with felony charges and sex offender registration when prosecutors allege the other person was a minor (Penal Law §§ 230.05, 230.06, 230.11-230.13). Forbes Stranger defends professionals, executives, tourists, and non-citizens arrested for solicitation and patronizing in every borough’s criminal court. When one arrest threatens your marriage, your career, and your reputation, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Promoting Prostitution and Sex Trafficking (PL §§ 230.15-230.34-a)

Promoting prostitution (Penal Law §§ 230.20-230.32) and sex trafficking (Penal Law §§ 230.34, 230.34-a) are New York crimes. They accuse someone of profiting from or helping run prostitution, such as through an escort service, massage business, website, or apartment, or of using force, deception, threats, drugs, or debt to make someone engage in commercial sex. It may mean New York prosecutors believe your business, your property, your driving, or your relationship with someone involved in sex work was part of a prostitution operation, and it can carry up to 25 years in state prison for sex trafficking or first-degree promoting, plus forfeiture of money and property and sex offender registration. Forbes Stranger defends business owners, spa and massage parlor operators, landlords, drivers, partners, and individuals in promoting prostitution and sex trafficking prosecutions. When prosecutors call you a trafficker, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Accused of Sexual Assault in College (Title IX)

A college sexual assault accusation is a complaint to a university’s Title IX office (20 U.S.C. § 1681) that a student engaged in sexual misconduct. New York colleges must apply an affirmative consent standard and follow the state’s Enough is Enough law (Education Law Article 129-B) in every investigation and hearing. It may mean the school believes an encounter at a party, in a dorm, or after a night of drinking was not consensual, and it can lead to suspension, expulsion, a permanent notation on your transcript (Education Law § 6444(6)), the loss of scholarships, visas, and graduate school admissions, and a parallel police investigation. Forbes Stranger defends college and graduate students, student-athletes, international students, and their families in Title IX investigations, campus hearings, and the criminal cases that can follow. When a campus accusation threatens a student’s education and freedom, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the school. Call Forbes Stranger now.

SORA Hearings: Level 1, 2, and 3 (Correction Law § 168-n)

A SORA hearing (Correction Law § 168-n) is the court proceeding that decides your risk level under New York’s Sex Offender Registration Act after a conviction, starting with a point score and recommendation from the Board of Examiners of Sex Offenders on its Risk Assessment Instrument. It may mean the Board or the District Attorney is asking the judge to assign a higher level than the facts support, and the level shapes the rest of your life: Registration duration and public disclosure depend on the assigned level and other legal requirements. Forbes Stranger represents individuals, professionals, and families at SORA risk level hearings in every borough. When a single hearing decides how the world will see you for life, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Lowering Your SORA Level or Getting Off the Registry (Correction Law § 168-o)

A SORA modification petition (Correction Law § 168-o) is a request to the court to lower your sex offender risk level, which you can file once a year, and certain Level 2 registrants can petition to end registration entirely after 30 years. It may mean that treatment, time, age, stable work and family ties, and a clean record since your conviction now show you pose a lower risk than when you were first classified, and a successful petition can take you off the public registry website, ease restrictions on where you live and work, and shorten how long you must register. Forbes Stranger represents Level 2 and Level 3 registrants and their families in SORA modification petitions and registry relief proceedings in every borough. When you are ready to move forward with your life, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Failure to Register as a Sex Offender (Correction Law § 168-t)

Failure to register (Correction Law § 168-t) is a New York crime that accuses a registered sex offender of not registering, not verifying their address, or not reporting a change of address, job, school, or internet account within 10 days, as SORA requires. It may mean police believe you moved, stayed somewhere new, missed an annual or 90-day verification, or left out an online account, and a first offense is a felony carrying up to four years in state prison, with up to seven years for a second offense and possible federal charges if you crossed state lines (18 U.S.C. § 2250). Forbes Stranger defends Level 1, 2, and 3 registrants, registrants living in shelters, and out-of-state registrants in failure to register prosecutions in every borough. When a paperwork deadline turns into a new felony, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Moving to New York as a Registered Sex Offender (Correction Law § 168-k)

Moving to New York with a prior sex-offender registration can create notification and registration obligations under New York law (Correction Law § 168-k). It may mean someone who was a low-tier registrant in another state is assigned Level 2 or Level 3 in New York, and the hearing that follows in the county where you live can bring lifetime registration and placement on the public registry. Forbes Stranger represents registrants relocating from other states, federal registrants, and service members with military convictions in SORA registration and risk level proceedings in every borough. When moving for work or family puts your future on the line, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Sex Offender Civil Commitment (Article 10) (Mental Hygiene Law Article 10)

Sex offender civil commitment, known in New York as Article 10 civil management (Mental Hygiene Law Article 10), is a proceeding brought by the Attorney General to keep someone convicted of a sex offense confined in a secure psychiatric treatment facility after their prison sentence ends, or under strict and intensive supervision and treatment (SIST). It may mean state evaluators have concluded you have a “mental abnormality” that makes you likely to reoffend, and if a jury agrees, you can be held indefinitely beyond your release date, with only annual reviews to win your freedom. Forbes Stranger represents incarcerated individuals, parolees, and their families in Article 10 probable cause hearings, jury trials, SIST proceedings, and annual reviews. When the State wants to keep you locked up after your sentence is served, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the State. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Where Sex Offenders Can Live: Parole and Housing Restrictions (Executive Law § 259-c(14))

New York’s Sexual Assault Reform Act, known as SARA (Executive Law § 259-c(14); Penal Law § 65.10(4-a)), bars people on parole, conditional release, or probation for certain sex offenses against minors, and all Level 3 registrants under supervision, from entering within 1,000 feet of a school. It may mean nearly every apartment in Manhattan and much of the outer boroughs is off-limits to you, and it can leave you held in prison past your release date, placed in a shelter, or violated and sent back to custody over where you live, work, or travel. Forbes Stranger represents parolees, probationers, registrants, and their families in SARA housing disputes, challenges to parole conditions, and parole violation hearings. When a 1,000-foot rule decides where you are allowed to live, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the parole board. Call Forbes Stranger now.
