Gun & Weapons Charges

Criminal Possession of a Weapon in the First Degree (CPW 1st, PL § 265.04)

Criminal possession of a weapon in the first degree (Penal Law § 265.04), often written as CPW 1st, is New York’s most serious weapons possession charge, and it accuses someone of possessing ten or more firearms or possessing an explosive substance with intent to use it unlawfully against a person or property.

It may mean police say they recovered a stockpile of guns from a home, storage unit, or vehicle, or found explosive material they claim you intended to use, and it is a class B violent felony carrying a mandatory minimum of five years and up to 25 years in state prison.

These cases often come with trafficking, conspiracy, or federal charges built around the same evidence.

Forbes Stranger defends individuals, licensed gun owners, collectors, and business owners in CPW 1st prosecutions across all five boroughs. When prosecutors charge you with New York’s most serious weapons possession crime, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Criminal Possession of a Weapon in the Second Degree (CPW 2nd, PL § 265.03)

Criminal possession of a weapon in the second degree (Penal Law § 265.03), often written as CPW 2nd, is a New York violent felony that accuses someone of possessing a loaded firearm outside their home or place of business, possessing five or more firearms, or possessing a loaded gun with intent to use it against another person.

It may mean police say they found a loaded handgun on you, in your bag, or in a car you were riding in, and it carries a mandatory minimum of three and a half years and up to 15 years in state prison, even for someone with no criminal record.

Many of these cases are won or lost on whether the stop, the search, or your statements were legal.

Forbes Stranger defends individuals, first-time offenders, licensed gun owners, out-of-state visitors, and young adults in CPW 2nd prosecutions across all five boroughs. When New York prosecutors are seeking a mandatory prison sentence, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Criminal Possession of a Weapon in the Third Degree (CPW 3rd, PL § 265.02)

Criminal possession of a weapon in the third degree (Penal Law § 265.02), often written as CPW 3rd, is a New York class D felony, with certain subdivisions designated as violent felonies. Charges may involve possessing a weapon after a prior conviction; possessing three or more firearms, a defaced firearm, an assault weapon, or a large-capacity magazine; or possessing explosive or incendiary devices.

It may mean police say they found a gun on you or in your home and that your record, the gun’s condition, or the number of guns makes it a felony, and it can carry up to seven years in state prison with violent-felony classification determined by the specific subdivision charged.

A prior conviction you thought was behind you can be what turns this charge into a felony.

Forbes Stranger defends individuals, gun owners, collectors, and people with prior records in CPW 3rd prosecutions across all five boroughs. When a gun charge threatens you with a violent felony record, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Criminal Possession of a Firearm (PL § 265.01-b)

Criminal possession of a firearm (Penal Law § 265.01-b) is a New York felony that accuses someone of possessing any firearm without a valid license, even an unloaded gun and even inside their own home.

It may mean police say they found an unlicensed handgun or another weapon meeting the Penal Law’s defined term “firearm” in your apartment, car, or belongings, and it is a class E felony that can carry up to four years in state prison and serious restrictions on future firearm possession and licensing, subject to applicable restoration and eligibility rules.

Many people charged with this crime are first-time offenders who did not realize that a gun they inherited, bought out of state, or kept at home for protection was illegal in New York City.

Forbes Stranger defends homeowners, first-time offenders, out-of-state gun owners, and people who inherited firearms in criminal possession of a firearm prosecutions across all five boroughs. When owning a gun for protection turns into a felony case, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Criminal Possession of a Weapon in the Fourth Degree (CPW 4th, PL § 265.01)

Criminal possession of a weapon in the fourth degree (Penal Law § 265.01), often written as CPW 4th, is a New York misdemeanor that accuses someone of possessing certain prohibited weapons, such as metal knuckles, billy clubs, or specific types of knives, or of possessing a knife or other dangerous instrument with intent to use it unlawfully against another person.

It may mean police say they found a weapon on you during a stop, a search, or an argument, and it can carry up to 364 days in jail, a criminal record, and immigration consequences for non-citizens.

What looks like a minor weapons charge becomes a felony if you have a prior conviction (Penal Law § 265.02(1)).

Forbes Stranger defends individuals, students, workers, and non-citizens in CPW 4th prosecutions in every borough’s criminal court. When a misdemeanor weapons charge threatens your record and your future, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

First-Time Gun Charges

A first-time gun charge is a New York weapons case brought against someone with no prior criminal record, usually criminal possession of a weapon in the second degree (Penal Law § 265.03) or criminal possession of a firearm (Penal Law § 265.01-b).

It may mean you have never been arrested before, yet prosecutors are treating you the same as someone with a record, and a CPW 2nd conviction still carries a mandatory minimum of three and a half years in state prison, even for a first offense.

Options like youthful offender treatment (Criminal Procedure Law Article 720), reduced pleas, and suppression of illegally obtained evidence must be pursued from the very first court date.

Forbes Stranger defends first-time offenders, students, professionals, out-of-state visitors, and young adults in gun prosecutions across all five boroughs. When your first arrest threatens years of your life, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Gun Charges With a Prior Record

A gun charge with a prior record is a New York weapons case brought against someone with an earlier conviction, which can turn a misdemeanor into a felony (Penal Law § 265.02(1)) and trigger enhanced sentencing as a second or persistent violent felony offender (Penal Law §§ 70.04, 70.08).

It may mean prosecutors are using your past to raise both the charge and the sentence, and a second violent felony conviction for CPW 2nd carries a mandatory minimum of seven years, while a persistent violent felony offender can face life in prison.

Whether a prior conviction legally counts, including its date, where it happened, and how it was obtained, can be challenged before it is used against you.

Forbes Stranger defends individuals with prior convictions, people on parole or probation, and those with out-of-state records in gun prosecutions across all five boroughs. When your record is being used to take more of your life, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Gun Found in Your Car

When police find a gun in a car, New York’s automobile presumption (Penal Law § 265.15(3)) creates a rebuttable evidentiary presumption of possession for certain vehicle occupants, subject to statutory exceptions, whether it was in the glove box, under a seat, or in a bag in the back.

It may mean you are facing CPW 2nd or another felony gun charge for a weapon you never touched or knew about, and it can carry a mandatory minimum of three and a half years in state prison if the gun was loaded.

The legality of the traffic stop and the exceptions to the car presumption can defeat the case if they are raised early.

Forbes Stranger defends drivers, passengers, rideshare drivers, and car owners in gun cases arising from traffic stops and vehicle searches across all five boroughs. When a gun in a car becomes your problem, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Gun Found in Your Home or Apartment

When police find a gun in a home or apartment, prosecutors may allege an occupant knowingly possessed it, even if the firearm was not found on that person. The charge may involve criminal possession of a firearm (Penal Law § 265.01-b) or criminal possession of a weapon in the third degree (Penal Law § 265.02).

It may mean a search warrant, a domestic call, or a parole visit turned up a gun in a shared bedroom, closet, or common area, and it can lead to felony charges for every adult in the household, along with eviction proceedings in NYCHA and other public housing.

Proving who actually controlled the space where the gun was found is often the difference between a conviction and a dismissal.

Forbes Stranger defends tenants, homeowners, family members, roommates, and NYCHA residents in gun cases arising from home searches across all five boroughs. When police find a gun where you live, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Gun Found During a Stop and Frisk

A stop and frisk is a street encounter in which police stop you, question you, and pat you down for weapons, and New York law allows a frisk only when officers reasonably suspect you are armed and dangerous (Criminal Procedure Law § 140.50; People v. De Bour).

It may mean police say they saw a bulge, a suspicious movement, or something in your waistband before stopping you, and if a loaded gun was recovered, it can lead to CPW 2nd charges with a mandatory minimum of three and a half years in state prison.

If the stop or the frisk was illegal, the gun can be suppressed and the case dismissed.

Forbes Stranger defends individuals, students, workers, and young adults in gun cases arising from street stops, frisks, and pat-downs across all five boroughs. When police stop first and justify it later, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Gun Found in Your Bag at JFK or LaGuardia

When TSA finds a gun in your carry-on or checked bag at JFK or LaGuardia, Port Authority police can arrest you under New York’s weapons laws, usually for criminal possession of a weapon in the second degree (Penal Law § 265.03) or criminal possession of a firearm (Penal Law § 265.01-b), even if the gun is legal where you live.

It may mean a gun you forgot was in your bag, or one you believed you were transporting legally, has turned a trip into a felony arrest, and it can bring a mandatory minimum of three and a half years in state prison if the gun was loaded, plus a separate TSA civil penalty that can reach thousands of dollars.

Defenses like the federal safe passage law (18 U.S.C. § 926A), and how the gun was packed and declared, must be raised immediately, often before you are even back home.

Forbes Stranger defends travelers, out-of-state gun owners, licensed carriers, and members of the military in airport gun cases at JFK and LaGuardia. When a forgotten gun turns a flight into a felony, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Out-of-State Gun Owners Arrested in New York

Out-of-state gun owners arrested in New York are usually charged with criminal possession of a weapon in the second degree (Penal Law § 265.03) or criminal possession of a firearm (Penal Law § 265.01-b), because New York does not honor carry permits from any other state, and New York City requires its own separate license (Penal Law § 400.00(6)).

It may mean you drove into the city, visited family, or traveled for work with a gun that is legally licensed where you live, and it can lead to a felony arrest, a mandatory minimum of three and a half years in state prison if the gun was loaded, and the loss of your license back home.

Your lawful ownership in another state, how the gun was stored, and federal transport protections can all shape the outcome if they are raised from the start.

Forbes Stranger defends gun owners from Pennsylvania, New Jersey, Connecticut, Florida, and every other state who have been arrested in New York City. When a gun that is legal at home becomes a felony in New York, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Licensed Gun Owners Facing Criminal Charges

Licensed gun owners in New York can still face criminal charges when they carry outside the limits of a premises or carry license, bring a gun into a sensitive location (Penal Law § 265.01-e), fail to store it safely (Penal Law § 265.45), or possess a firearm not listed on their NYPD license.

It may mean a routine traffic stop, a domestic dispute, or an unrelated arrest exposed a technical license violation, and it can lead to felony weapons charges, the immediate suspension of your license, and the surrender of every gun you own.

The criminal case and your NYPD License Division proceeding move at the same time, and what you say in one can be used in the other.

Forbes Stranger defends premises license holders, carry license holders, business owners, security professionals, and retired officers in criminal cases involving their licensed firearms. When doing everything right still ends in an arrest, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Guns in Sensitive Places: Subway, Times Square, and More

Carrying a gun in a sensitive place (Penal Law § 265.01-e) is a New York felony that applies even to people with valid carry licenses, and it covers the subway, buses, Times Square, parks, schools, hospitals, houses of worship, bars and restaurants that serve alcohol, stadiums, theaters, and many other locations.

It may mean you were stopped with a licensed gun on the subway, at a concert, or while walking through Times Square, and it can lead to a felony conviction, up to four years in state prison, and the permanent loss of your carry license.

Whether the location actually qualifies, whether an exception applies, and how the law has fared in ongoing constitutional challenges can all affect your case.

Forbes Stranger defends licensed carriers, security professionals, retired officers, and out-of-state visitors charged with carrying in sensitive locations across New York City. When carrying legally in one place becomes a felony a block away, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

NYPD Gun License Suspended, Revoked, or Denied

An NYPD gun license suspension, revocation, or denial is a decision by the NYPD License Division to take away or refuse your handgun license or rifle and shotgun permit, governed by New York’s licensing law (Penal Law § 400.00) and the NYPD’s own rules (38 RCNY Chapters 3 and 5).

It may mean an arrest, an order of protection, a domestic incident, or even an anonymous complaint led the License Division to act, and it can require you to surrender every firearm you own immediately and permanently end your ability to legally own a gun in New York City.

The License Division hearing is your best chance to keep your license, with a court challenge under CPLR Article 78 available only after that decision is final.

Forbes Stranger represents premises and carry license holders, license applicants, business owners, and security professionals before the NYPD License Division and in Article 78 proceedings. When the NYPD comes for your license and your guns, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the NYPD. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Red Flag Orders (Extreme Risk Protection Orders)

A red flag order, officially an Extreme Risk Protection Order (CPLR Article 63-A), is a court order that bars you from possessing or buying any firearm because police, a prosecutor, a family member, a school official, or a health care provider claims you are a danger to yourself or others.

It may mean a judge has already signed a temporary order without hearing from you, and it can require the immediate surrender of every gun you own and, if a final order is entered, keep you from owning a firearm for up to a year, with renewals possible.

The hearing on a final order is held within days, where the petitioner must prove its case by clear and convincing evidence.

Forbes Stranger defends gun owners, licensed carriers, veterans, and family members in red flag and Extreme Risk Protection Order proceedings across all five boroughs. When a single accusation threatens your guns and your rights, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the government. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Ghost Guns and 3D-Printed Guns

Ghost gun and 3D-printed gun charges are New York crimes that accuse someone of possessing, building, or selling an unserialized firearm, frame, or receiver (Penal Law §§ 265.01(9)-(10), 265.07, 265.60-265.64), or, under a 2026 law, of knowingly distributing digital firearm manufacturing code to an unqualified recipient under § 265.10(11), or possessing such code with one of the specified unlawful-manufacture or distribution intents under § 265.10(12), subject to statutory exceptions.

It may mean police or federal agents tracked a parts kit purchase, a 3D printer, or files on your computer, and it can lead to misdemeanor or felony charges for possession, felony charges for manufacturing or selling, and federal charges built on the same evidence.

These laws are new, technical, and still being tested in court, so how the government proves what you built and what you intended can decide the case.

Forbes Stranger defends hobbyists, engineers, students, gun owners, and online sellers in ghost gun and 3D-printed firearm prosecutions across all five boroughs. When a printer or a parts kit becomes the center of a criminal case, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Assault Weapons and High-Capacity Magazines

Possessing an assault weapon, or a high-capacity magazine (legally, a large-capacity ammunition feeding device that holds more than ten rounds), is criminal possession of a weapon in the third degree (Penal Law § 265.02(7)-(8)), a violent felony under New York’s SAFE Act.

It may mean police say a rifle, pistol, or magazine you own has features or capacity banned in New York, even if it was bought legally in another state, and it can carry up to seven years in state prison and a violent felony record.

Whether a gun actually meets the legal definition of an assault weapon (Penal Law § 265.00(22)) often comes down to technical features an expert must examine.

Forbes Stranger defends gun owners, collectors, veterans, out-of-state residents, and licensed dealers in assault weapon and high-capacity magazine prosecutions across all five boroughs. When a rifle or magazine bought legally elsewhere becomes a violent felony in New York, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Ammunition and Bulletproof Vests

Ammunition and bulletproof vest charges are New York City and New York State crimes that accuse someone of possessing ammunition without a valid NYC license (NYC Administrative Code § 10-131), buying or selling body armor without being in an eligible profession (Penal Law §§ 270.21, 270.22), or wearing a body vest while allegedly committing a violent crime (Penal Law § 270.20).

It may mean police found bullets in your car, bag, or home, or a vest you bought online, and it can lead to criminal charges of its own, while a body armor count added to a gun case can raise both the charges and the prison exposure.

Many people charged with these crimes had no idea that purchases legal elsewhere are illegal in New York City.

Forbes Stranger defends gun owners, security professionals, out-of-state visitors, and individuals in ammunition and body armor prosecutions across all five boroughs. When a box of bullets or a vest becomes a criminal case, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Knife and Brass Knuckle Charges

Knife and brass knuckle charges are New York crimes that accuse someone of possessing metal knuckles or other banned weapons, carrying a knife with intent to use it unlawfully (Penal Law § 265.01), or, in New York City, carrying a knife with a blade four inches or longer in public (NYC Administrative Code § 10-133).

It may mean police stopped you and found a work knife, a pocketknife, or brass knuckles on your keychain, and it can lead to a misdemeanor, a criminal record, and, if you have a prior conviction, a felony charge.

Whether the item is actually illegal, and whether police had the right to search you, can decide the case.

Forbes Stranger defends workers, tradespeople, students, and individuals in knife and brass knuckle prosecutions in every borough’s criminal court. When an everyday tool becomes a weapons charge, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

BB Guns, Airsoft, and Fake Guns

BB gun, airsoft, and fake gun charges are New York City crimes that accuse someone of possessing an air pistol, air rifle, or realistic-looking imitation firearm, all of which are banned in the city unless they meet strict color and design rules (NYC Administrative Code § 10-131).

It may mean police mistook a BB gun, airsoft gun, or replica for a real firearm during a stop, and it can lead to criminal charges, a dangerous confrontation with officers, and far more serious charges if prosecutors claim it was displayed during an argument or a robbery.

The difference between a toy, a replica, and a real firearm must be proven before a misunderstanding becomes a conviction.

Forbes Stranger defends teenagers, students, hobbyists, and collectors in BB gun, airsoft, and imitation firearm prosecutions in every borough’s criminal court. When a toy or replica is treated like a real gun, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Criminal Sale of a Firearm and Gun Trafficking

Criminal sale of a firearm (Penal Law §§ 265.11-265.14) is a New York violent felony that accuses someone of selling, trading, or giving away guns unlawfully, and the charge grows more serious with the number of guns sold, reaching first-degree criminal sale for ten or more firearms unlawfully disposed of, or three or more firearms disposed of to others within one year, under Penal Law § 265.13.

It may mean prosecutors believe you helped bring guns into New York City along the “Iron Pipeline” from other states, often after months of undercover buys and recorded calls, and it can carry up to 25 years in state prison, with federal trafficking charges often brought on the same evidence.

These cases usually involve multiple defendants, cooperating witnesses, and wiretaps that must be challenged one by one.

Forbes Stranger defends individuals, licensed dealers, buyers, and alleged members of trafficking rings in criminal sale of a firearm and gun trafficking prosecutions across all five boroughs. When prosecutors call you a gun trafficker, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Straw Purchases

A straw purchase, charged in New York as criminal purchase or disposal of a weapon (Penal Law § 265.17), accuses someone of buying a gun for a person who is legally barred from owning one, or of buying or transferring a firearm while knowing the buyer is prohibited.

It may mean investigators traced a gun recovered in a crime back to a purchase made in your name, often in another state, and it can lead to a felony charge in New York and a separate federal straw purchasing charge (18 U.S.C. § 932) carrying up to 15 years in prison.

Agents often show up asking about a gun you bought years ago, hoping you will explain where it went before you call a lawyer.

Forbes Stranger defends gun buyers, family members, partners, and friends accused of straw purchases in New York State and federal prosecutions. When a gun you once bought turns up in someone else’s case, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Teen Arrested With a Gun

When a teenager is arrested with a gun in New York, a 16- or 17-year-old is charged as an adolescent offender in the Youth Part of Supreme Court under Raise the Age (Criminal Procedure Law Article 722), and younger children may face Family Court or adult charges depending on the crime and their age.

It may mean your son or daughter is facing CPW 2nd or another felony gun charge, and it can bring detention, a criminal record, school discipline, and the possibility of an adult prison sentence if the case stays in criminal court.

The fight to move the case to Family Court or win youthful offender treatment (Criminal Procedure Law Article 720) starts at the very first appearance.

Forbes Stranger defends teenagers and young adults charged with gun possession. When your child’s future is on the line, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between them and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.
