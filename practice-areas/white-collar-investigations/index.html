White-Collar Investigations

Fraud, tax, and financial investigations.

Investigations and proceedings where the record is financial — and the analysis has to meet it.

White-collar matters are usually decided on documents. Financial records, corporate structures, and the paper trail behind a transaction carry more weight than narrative, and the defense is strongest when it is built by someone who can read the record as an investigator would. The firm’s approach draws on forensic accounting experience and on prosecution-side insight into how such cases are assembled.

Investigations before charges

Grand jury subpoenas, regulatory requests, and internal reviews all present the same early question: what should be produced, by whom, and on what timetable. The firm advises companies and individuals at that stage, before positions have hardened and while the record can still be organised deliberately.

Reading the record

Helena Veronique Stranger’s background includes forensic accounting and investigations at PwC, where she conducted fraud and financial investigations involving major corporate clients. That experience informs the firm’s analysis of financial records, corporate structures, documentary evidence, and investigative methods — the material on which white-collar cases turn.

Internal and parallel proceedings

Internal investigations, regulatory action, and criminal exposure often proceed at the same time, and each can affect the others. The firm coordinates a single consistent position across forums rather than treating them as separate problems.

Cross-border matters

With admission in New York and Germany, the firm handles matters for international clients and investigations that cross legal systems. In Germany, the practice has included fraud, tax offenses, corporate misconduct, and complex investigations for international clients.

Discretion

The firm has handled high-profile, press-covered matters. Those engagements are conducted on the assumption that confidentiality is part of the representation, not a courtesy extended after the fact.
