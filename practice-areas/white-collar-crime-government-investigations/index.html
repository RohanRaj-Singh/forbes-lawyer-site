White Collar Crime & Government Investigations

Grand Larceny and Embezzlement

Grand larceny (Penal Law §§ 155.30-155.42) is a New York crime that accuses someone of stealing property worth more than $1,000, or stealing specified property or by specified means regardless of that dollar threshold, including by embezzlement, false pretenses, or false promises (Penal Law § 155.05).

It may mean New York prosecutors believe money you handled for an employer, client, business partner, or investor was taken or misused, and it can carry up to 25 years in state prison when the alleged loss exceeds $1 million, plus restitution.

The degree of the charge, and your prison exposure, turns on a dollar amount the prosecution calculates.

Forbes Stranger defends executives, employees, bookkeepers, financial advisers, business partners, and fiduciaries in grand larceny and embezzlement prosecutions. When New York prosecutors accuse you of stealing from people who trusted you, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Identity Theft and Cybercrime

Identity theft (Penal Law §§ 190.78-190.80) and computer crimes (Penal Law Article 156) are New York crimes that accuse someone of using another person’s personal identifying information to obtain money, credit, goods, or services, or of accessing, altering, or stealing from computers, accounts, or data without authorization.

It may mean New York prosecutors believe stolen names, account numbers, or login credentials were used in a transaction or traced back to your devices, and it can carry up to seven years in state prison for first-degree identity theft, with more when computer tampering or larceny charges are added.

These cases often begin with a search warrant for your phone, computer, and accounts.

Forbes Stranger defends individuals, employees, students, business owners, and technology professionals in identity theft, credit card fraud, and computer crime prosecutions. When the prosecution says the digital trail leads to you, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Forgery and Check Fraud

Forgery (Penal Law §§ 170.05-170.15) and criminal possession of a forged instrument (Penal Law §§ 170.20-170.30) are New York crimes that accuse someone of making, altering, signing, or knowingly possessing a fake or altered document, including checks, deeds, contracts, and government records, with intent to defraud.

It may mean New York prosecutors believe a check you deposited, a signature you provided, or a document you used was forged or altered, and it can carry up to seven years in state prison for second-degree forgery and up to 15 years for first-degree forgery, often with grand larceny charges added.

A single deposited check can lead to felony charges, frozen accounts, and bank records already in the prosecution’s hands.

Forbes Stranger defends individuals, business owners, employees, and account holders in forgery, check fraud, and forged instrument prosecutions. When a forged signature or a bad check becomes a felony case, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Scheme to Defraud

Scheme to defraud (Penal Law §§ 190.60, 190.65) is a New York crime that accuses someone of engaging in a systematic ongoing scheme meeting one of the alternative statutory tests, including an intent to defraud ten or more persons and obtaining property from at least one, or an intent to defraud more than one person and obtaining over $1,000. Other statutory theories may also apply.

It may mean New York prosecutors believe your business practices, sales pitches, or investment offerings misled customers or investors, and it can carry up to four years in state prison for first-degree scheme to defraud, often charged alongside grand larceny counts that carry far more.

Prosecutors can build these cases from complaints, bank records, and customer statements gathered long before you are contacted.

Forbes Stranger defends business owners, executives, salespeople, contractors, and investment promoters in scheme to defraud prosecutions. When New York prosecutors call your business a scheme, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Falsifying Business Records

Falsifying business records (Penal Law §§ 175.05, 175.10) is a New York crime that accuses someone of making a false entry in a company’s records, or altering, deleting, or omitting information, with intent to defraud.

It may mean New York prosecutors believe an invoice, ledger, payroll record, or internal report was changed to hide another crime or mislead regulators, investors, or auditors, and it can carry up to four years in state prison per felony count, and multiple false entries can result in multiple felony charges.

A handful of bookkeeping entries can become dozens of felony counts.

Forbes Stranger defends executives, CFOs, accountants, bookkeepers, compliance officers, and business owners in falsifying business records prosecutions. When New York prosecutors turn your company’s books into evidence, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Insurance Fraud

Insurance fraud (Penal Law Article 176) is a New York crime that accuses someone of submitting false or misleading information to an insurer, including in auto, no-fault, workers’ compensation, health, life, and property claims, or in applications for coverage.

It may mean New York prosecutors believe a claim, a medical bill, a payroll report, or an accident was inflated, staged, or misrepresented, and it can carry up to 25 years in state prison when the alleged fraud exceeds $1 million.

Insurers’ special investigations units often spend months building a file before referring it to prosecutors.

Forbes Stranger defends medical providers, clinic owners, business owners, contractors, attorneys, and claimants in insurance fraud prosecutions. When an insurer’s accusation becomes a criminal case, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Deed Theft, Real Estate, and Landlord Fraud

Deed theft, residential mortgage fraud (Penal Law Article 187), and false housing filings (Penal Law § 175.35) are New York crimes that accuse someone of using deception to take, transfer, finance, or profit from real property, and deed theft is now prosecuted as grand larceny (Penal Law §§ 155.30-155.42).

It may mean New York prosecutors believe a deed transfer, mortgage application, rent registration, or building filing was false or obtained by fraud, and it can carry up to 25 years in state prison when the property involved is worth more than $1 million, which in New York City it often is.

Your property, title, and financing can be frozen the moment a case is filed.

Forbes Stranger defends real estate investors, landlords, property managers, brokers, attorneys, title agents, and family members in deed theft, mortgage fraud, and landlord fraud prosecutions. When a property dispute becomes a criminal case, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Health Care and Medicaid Fraud

Health care fraud (Penal Law Article 177) is a New York crime that accuses someone of submitting false claims to Medicaid, Medicare, or a private health plan, including for services that were never provided, were billed at inflated rates, or were not medically necessary.

It may mean the Attorney General’s Medicaid Fraud Control Unit or a District Attorney believes your billing, documentation, or home care records crossed the line into fraud, and the most serious charges can carry up to 25 years in state prison, plus restitution and possible exclusion from Medicaid.

Audits, payment holds, and subpoenas from the Office of the Medicaid Inspector General often arrive before any charge is filed.

Forbes Stranger defends physicians, pharmacists, nurses, home care agency owners, clinic owners, and health care executives in health care and Medicaid fraud prosecutions. When New York prosecutors come after your practice or your license, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Wage Theft and Construction Fraud

Wage theft and construction fraud are New York crimes that accuse an employer or contractor of underpaying workers, falsifying certified payrolls (Penal Law § 175.35), cheating prevailing wage requirements, or hiding payroll to cut workers’ compensation premiums, and wage theft is now prosecuted as larceny (Penal Law § 155.05).

It may mean New York prosecutors believe your payroll records, subcontractor arrangements, or job site practices shortchanged workers or the government, and because unpaid wages can be added together across every worker, a payroll dispute can become a felony grand larceny charge carrying years in state prison.

Investigators often interview your workers and subpoena your payroll records before you know a case exists.

Forbes Stranger defends contractors, subcontractors, developers, restaurant owners, and business owners in wage theft and construction fraud prosecutions. When New York prosecutors come after your business and your payroll, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Elder Financial Exploitation

Elder financial exploitation is prosecuted in New York as grand larceny (Penal Law §§ 155.30-155.42) and accuses someone of taking or misusing an older person’s money or property, often through a power of attorney, joint bank account, caregiving relationship, or investment advice.

It may mean New York prosecutors believe withdrawals, gifts, transfers, or changes to accounts were obtained through deception or an abuse of trust, and it can carry up to 25 years in state prison when the alleged loss exceeds $1 million.

These cases often begin with a bank’s suspicious activity report or a family member’s complaint, long before anyone asks for your side.

Forbes Stranger defends adult children, relatives, caregivers, home health aides, financial advisers, and attorneys in elder financial exploitation prosecutions. When a family dispute over money becomes a criminal case, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

New York Tax Fraud

New York tax fraud (Tax Law §§ 1801-1806) is a crime that accuses someone of willfully filing false returns, failing to file, evading state or city income tax, or collecting sales or payroll taxes and failing to pay them over to the state.

It may mean New York prosecutors and the Department of Taxation and Finance believe your returns, residency claims, or business’s sales tax filings show intentional deception, and it can carry up to 25 years in state prison when the alleged tax loss exceeds $1 million, plus back taxes, interest, and penalties.

A routine state audit can be referred to criminal investigators without warning.

Forbes Stranger defends business owners, restaurant and retail owners, executives, high-net-worth individuals, and tax preparers in New York tax fraud prosecutions. When New York State stops auditing and starts prosecuting, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Martin Act Securities Fraud

The Martin Act (General Business Law § 352-c) is New York’s securities fraud law, and it accuses someone of using fraud, deception, misrepresentation, or false promises to sell or promote stocks, bonds, investment funds, commodities, or real estate securities.

It may mean the Attorney General or a District Attorney believes your investment offering, fund, or sales pitch misled New York investors, and because some Martin Act violations require no proof that you intended to defraud anyone, it is one of the most powerful prosecution tools in the country, often paired with grand larceny counts carrying up to 25 years in state prison.

The Attorney General can subpoena your documents and testimony before any charge is filed.

Forbes Stranger defends fund managers, brokers, investment advisers, startup founders, real estate syndicators, and executives in Martin Act prosecutions and investigations. When New York’s most powerful fraud statute is aimed at you, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Money Laundering

Money laundering (Penal Law Article 470) is a New York crime that accuses someone of conducting financial transactions with money from criminal conduct, knowing its source, with a qualifying statutory purpose or knowledge, including promoting criminal conduct, concealing the nature or source of proceeds, or avoiding transaction-reporting requirements, and with the applicable monetary threshold.

It may mean New York prosecutors believe money that moved through your accounts, your business, or your real estate deals was the proceeds of a crime, and it can carry up to 25 years in state prison when the transactions exceed $1 million, plus forfeiture of the funds.

Prosecutors can move to freeze your accounts and property before you are ever charged.

Forbes Stranger defends business owners, real estate investors, financial professionals, money service operators, and individuals in New York money laundering prosecutions. When New York prosecutors follow the money to you, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Bribery, Official Misconduct, and Public Corruption

Bribery (Penal Law Article 200), official misconduct (Penal Law § 195.00), and defrauding the government (Penal Law § 195.20) are New York crimes that accuse someone of offering, giving, or accepting a benefit in exchange for official action, or of a public servant abusing their position for personal gain.

It may mean New York prosecutors or the NYC Department of Investigation believe a payment, contract, gift, permit, or vote was really a bribe or a favor, and it can carry up to 25 years in state prison for first-degree bribery, along with removal from office and, in some cases, the loss of your pension.

These cases often break publicly in the press before you ever see a courtroom.

Forbes Stranger defends public officials, city and state employees, contractors, developers, lobbyists, and business owners in bribery, official misconduct, and public corruption prosecutions. When New York prosecutors put your office, your business, and your reputation on the line, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Public Benefits and Unemployment Fraud

Public benefits and unemployment fraud can involve false statements or concealed income used to obtain unemployment insurance, SNAP, public assistance, Medicaid benefits, or housing assistance.

It may mean New York prosecutors believe your applications, certifications, or reported income did not match your actual work or household situation, and it can bring criminal charges, financial penalties, and repayment obligations.

The agency that paid the benefits has usually built its case from your own records before referring it to prosecutors.

Forbes Stranger defends individuals, families, workers, and small business owners in public benefits, welfare, and unemployment insurance fraud prosecutions. When a benefits claim becomes a criminal case, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Trademark Counterfeiting

Trademark counterfeiting (Penal Law §§ 165.71-165.73) is a New York crime that accuses someone of manufacturing, importing, distributing, selling, or possessing for sale goods that carry a fake brand name or logo, with intent to deceive.

It may mean New York prosecutors believe merchandise in your store, warehouse, or online shop was counterfeit, and it can carry up to 15 years in state prison when the retail value of the goods exceeds $100,000, plus seizure of your inventory and proceeds.

These cases often start with a raid that seizes your inventory, cash, and records in a single day.

Forbes Stranger defends store owners, importers, wholesalers, online sellers, and street vendors in trademark counterfeiting prosecutions. When a brand’s complaint becomes a criminal case, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Perjury, Evidence Tampering, and Obstruction

Perjury (Penal Law Article 210), evidence and witness tampering (Penal Law Article 215), and obstructing governmental administration (Penal Law § 195.05) are New York crimes that accuse someone of lying under oath, destroying or hiding evidence, influencing a witness, or interfering with an investigation or proceeding.

It may mean New York prosecutors believe your grand jury testimony, sworn statement, document production, or contact with a witness was meant to mislead them, and it can carry up to seven years in state prison for first-degree perjury, even if the original investigation goes nowhere.

Every new statement, deleted file, or conversation with a witness can become another charge.

Forbes Stranger defends executives, employees, business owners, professionals, and public officials in perjury, tampering, and obstruction prosecutions. When prosecutors turn your response to an investigation into a new crime, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

District Attorney Investigations and Grand Jury Subpoenas

A District Attorney investigation is a state criminal inquiry run by one of New York City’s five District Attorney’s offices, and a grand jury subpoena (Criminal Procedure Law Article 610) is a legal order requiring you or your company to produce documents or testify before a state grand jury.

It may mean New York prosecutors are building a case against you or someone you work with, and what you produce, what you say, and whether you sign a waiver of immunity (Criminal Procedure Law § 190.40) can decide whether you leave the process as a witness or a defendant.

Subpoena deadlines are short and leave no room for mistakes.

Forbes Stranger defends individuals, executives, companies, records custodians, and professionals in District Attorney investigations and state grand jury proceedings. When a New York District Attorney comes looking for you, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Grand Jury Notice and Testimony

Grand jury notice (Criminal Procedure Law § 190.50) is the prosecution’s notice that it will ask a New York grand jury to indict you, but the prosecution’s duty to give notice generally depends on a pending undisposed felony complaint, and the defendant must timely serve a written request and execute a waiver of immunity to testify.

It may mean New York prosecutors are ready to seek a felony indictment within days, and testifying requires signing a waiver of immunity, so everything you say under oath can be used against you at trial.

The decision to testify or stay silent must be made fast and cannot be undone.

Forbes Stranger defends executives, business owners, professionals, and individuals facing New York grand jury presentations. When a grand jury is about to decide your future, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

New York Attorney General Investigations

A New York Attorney General investigation is an inquiry by the state’s top law enforcement office, often under Executive Law § 63(12) or the Martin Act, into alleged fraud or illegality in how a business, fund, nonprofit, or individual operates.

It may mean the Attorney General believes your business engaged in repeated fraudulent or illegal conduct, and it can lead to subpoenas for your documents and testimony, multimillion-dollar penalties and disgorgement, a bar from doing business in New York, and referral for criminal prosecution.

Testimony you give in a civil investigation can be used against you in a criminal case.

Forbes Stranger defends executives, business owners, fund managers, real estate developers, nonprofit leaders, and companies in New York Attorney General investigations. When the Attorney General of New York puts your business under the microscope, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

NYC DOI and NYS Inspector General Investigations

The NYC Department of Investigation (New York City Charter Chapter 34) and the New York State Inspector General (Executive Law Article 4-A) investigate fraud, corruption, and misconduct involving city and state agencies, their employees, and the contractors and vendors that do business with them.

It may mean investigators believe you misused public funds, took a benefit you were not entitled to, or helped a contractor or vendor cheat the government, and it can lead to termination, debarment from public contracts, and referral to a District Attorney for criminal prosecution.

Public employees can be ordered to answer investigators’ questions or lose their jobs.

Forbes Stranger defends city and state employees, supervisors, contractors, vendors, and business owners in DOI and Inspector General investigations. When the government investigates its own employees and contractors, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

NY DFS Investigations

A New York Department of Financial Services investigation (Financial Services Law § 404) is an inquiry by the state regulator of banks, insurers, money transmitters, and cryptocurrency companies into alleged fraud, compliance failures, or violations of New York’s banking, insurance, anti-money laundering, and cybersecurity rules.

It may mean DFS believes your company or your conduct broke New York financial rules, and it can lead to multimillion-dollar penalties, license revocation, independent monitors, personal bars from the industry, and referral to prosecutors through DFS’s own Criminal Investigations Bureau.

What you tell a regulator can become evidence in a criminal case.

Forbes Stranger defends executives, compliance officers, insurance professionals, bank employees, cryptocurrency founders, and financial companies in DFS investigations. When New York’s financial regulator comes after your license and your business, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Asset Freezes and CPLR Article 13-A Forfeiture

CPLR Article 13-A is New York’s civil forfeiture law, and it allows a District Attorney to freeze, seize, and ultimately keep money and property the prosecution claims are the proceeds or instruments of a crime, often through court orders obtained without notice to you.

It may mean New York prosecutors believe your bank accounts, real estate, business assets, or cash are tied to an alleged fraud, and it can leave you unable to pay employees, run your business, cover your family’s expenses, or pay for your defense.

Every day the freeze stays in place costs you money you may never recover.

Forbes Stranger defends business owners, executives, investors, family members, and companies in CPLR Article 13-A forfeiture actions and asset freeze proceedings. When New York prosecutors freeze everything you have, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Professional License Investigations

Professional license investigations are proceedings by the Office of Professional Medical Conduct (Public Health Law § 230) or the State Education Department’s Office of the Professions (Education Law § 6510) into alleged misconduct by physicians, nurses, pharmacists, accountants, and other licensed professionals.

It may mean regulators believe your billing, prescribing, record-keeping, or a criminal charge against you reflects professional misconduct, and it can lead to suspension, revocation, fines, and public discipline that follows your name permanently.

What you say in the criminal case and the licensing case can be used against you in both.

Forbes Stranger defends physicians, nurses, pharmacists, dentists, accountants, and other licensed professionals in license investigations and the criminal cases connected to them. When a single investigation threatens both your freedom and your career, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Fraud Victim Representation

Fraud victim representation is the work of building and presenting a criminal referral to prosecutors on behalf of people and companies who have been defrauded, then protecting their rights to be heard and repaid as the case moves forward (Executive Law § 641; Penal Law § 60.27).

It may mean the money you lost can be recovered through restitution in a criminal case, but prosecutors decide which cases to bring, and a fraud that is not documented and presented the right way can go uncharged.

The evidence, the assets, and the people who took them can disappear quickly.

Forbes Stranger represents individuals, families, investors, businesses, and nonprofits who have been victims of fraud, embezzlement, deed theft, and investment schemes.
