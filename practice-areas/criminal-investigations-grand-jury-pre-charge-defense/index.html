Criminal Investigations, Grand Juries & Pre-Charge Defense

NYPD Detective Wants to Talk to You

An NYPD detective asking you to come to the precinct, call back, or “clear something up” is requesting a voluntary police interview, and detectives do not have to read you your rights before one begins.

It may mean you are a suspect, or that detectives already have enough to arrest you and want your statement first, and it can turn a single conversation into the evidence used to charge and convict you.

Walking into the precinct to explain yourself can end with you leaving in handcuffs.

Forbes Stranger defends individuals, professionals, students, and families in NYPD investigations across all five boroughs. When a detective wants to talk, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Detective Left a Card at Your Door

A detective’s business card left at your door, with your family, or at your job is the NYPD’s way of asking you to call back, and it usually means detectives have already identified you in an open investigation.

It may mean there is an I-Card or a warrant for your arrest, or that detectives are building a case and want your side on the record, and it can lead to an arrest at home, at work, or on the street if the card is ignored or handled the wrong way.

The call you make next can shape whether, when, and how an arrest happens.

Forbes Stranger defends individuals, professionals, students, and families who have been contacted by NYPD detectives in all five boroughs. When a detective’s card shows up at your door, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Contacted by a Special Victims Detective

A call from a detective in the NYPD Special Victims Division means you are part of a sex crime or child abuse investigation, often based on an allegation made by someone you know.

It may mean someone has accused you of sexual misconduct or abuse and detectives are gathering evidence before deciding whether to arrest, and it can bring felony charges, sex offender registration under SORA (Correction Law Article 6-C), and damage to your family, career, and reputation from the accusation alone.

Detectives often gather recorded calls, messages, and DNA before you ever hear the full allegation.

Forbes Stranger defends individuals, professionals, educators, students, and parents in Special Victims investigations across all five boroughs. When Special Victims detectives come looking for you, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

FBI or Federal Agents at Your Door

A visit or call from the FBI, Homeland Security Investigations, the DEA, the ATF, IRS Criminal Investigation, the Secret Service, or Postal Inspectors is a request for a federal interview, and it usually comes without warning at your home, your office, or on your phone.

It may mean you are a target, a subject, or a witness in a federal investigation that has been running for months, and knowingly making a materially false statement to federal agents can result in separate criminal charges (18 U.S.C. § 1001).

Agents want to talk before you have a lawyer, while you are surprised and unprepared.

Forbes Stranger defends individuals, executives, professionals, and families contacted by federal agents in New York. When federal agents knock on your door, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Falsely Accused of a Crime

Being falsely accused of a crime means someone has reported, or is threatening to report, that you committed an offense you did not commit, often after a breakup, a custody fight, a business dispute, or a workplace conflict.

It may mean police and prosecutors are treating the accusation as true until the evidence proves otherwise, and it can lead to your arrest, an order of protection that removes you from your own home, and consequences that follow you even after the case is dismissed.

The evidence that proves your innocence, like texts, video, and witnesses, can disappear fast.

Forbes Stranger defends individuals, professionals, parents, students, and business owners against false accusations before and after charges are filed. When a lie threatens your freedom, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Warrant for Your Arrest or NYPD I-Card

An arrest warrant (Criminal Procedure Law Article 120) is a court order directing police to arrest you, and an NYPD I-Card is an internal alert telling every officer in the city that a detective wants you picked up, often with no judge involved at all.

It may mean detectives believe they have probable cause to arrest you, and it can lead to an arrest at home, at work, or during a routine traffic stop, followed by a night in Central Booking before you ever see a judge.

An unexpected arrest takes away your chance to surrender on your own terms and prepare for arraignment.

Forbes Stranger defends individuals, professionals, students, and families facing open warrants and I-Cards across all five boroughs. When the NYPD is looking for you, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Turning Yourself In

Turning yourself in, known as a voluntary surrender, is an arranged arrest in which your lawyer coordinates with the detective or prosecutor so you report to the precinct or court at a set time instead of being picked up without warning.

It may mean police already have probable cause or a warrant to arrest you, and how the surrender is handled can affect whether you are released at arraignment, how long you spend in custody, and what you say along the way.

Walking into a precinct without counsel invites questioning, delays, and a harder fight for release.

Forbes Stranger arranges voluntary surrenders and defends individuals, professionals, students, and families at every precinct and courthouse in New York City. When it is time to face the charges, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Loved One Arrested and in Central Booking

Central Booking is where a person arrested in New York City is held after the precinct, waiting to be charged and brought before a judge for arraignment, which by law must happen without unnecessary delay (Criminal Procedure Law § 140.20).

It may mean the District Attorney is deciding right now what charges to file, and it can lead to a judge setting bail, ordering release, or issuing an order of protection within the first 24 hours.

Decisions on charges, bail, and release happen fast, often in the middle of the night.

Forbes Stranger represents people who have been arrested and guides their families through Central Booking and arraignment in Manhattan, Brooklyn, Queens, the Bronx, and Staten Island. When someone you love is in Central Booking, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between them and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Desk Appearance Tickets

A Desk Appearance Ticket (Criminal Procedure Law Article 150) is a written order to appear in criminal court for arraignment on a future date, issued after an arrest when police release you from the precinct instead of holding you.

It may mean you have been arrested for a misdemeanor or lower-level felony and the District Attorney is now deciding what to charge, and it can still lead to a criminal record, an order of protection, and immigration or employment consequences, while missing the court date brings a warrant for your arrest.

The weeks before your court date are the best time to convince prosecutors to reduce or decline the case.

Forbes Stranger defends individuals, professionals, students, and non-citizens with Desk Appearance Tickets in every borough’s criminal court. When a small ticket carries big consequences, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Wanted in Another State

Being wanted in another state means a warrant has been issued for you outside New York, and if you are found or arrested here, you can be held as a fugitive and sent back through interstate extradition (Criminal Procedure Law Article 570).

It may mean another state has charged you, issued a bench warrant, or claims you violated probation or parole, and it can lead to your arrest in New York, weeks in jail waiting for the other state to come get you, and a case fought far from home.

The decision to waive or fight extradition, and whether you are released while you wait, is made quickly in a New York courtroom.

Forbes Stranger defends individuals arrested in New York on out-of-state warrants. When another state wants you, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Police Search Warrant or Raid

A search warrant (New York Criminal Procedure Law Article 690 for state warrants; Fed. R. Crim. P. 41 for federal warrants) is a court order allowing police or federal agents to enter your home, office, car, or storage space and seize evidence, and it is often carried out early in the morning, without warning, by a team of officers.

It may mean investigators have already convinced a judge there is probable cause that evidence of a crime is inside, and it can lead to the seizure of your phones, computers, cash, and records, arrests on the spot, and charges built on whatever is found.

The legality of the warrant and how it was carried out may be your strongest defense, but only if it is challenged early.

Forbes Stranger defends individuals, families, business owners, and professionals whose homes and businesses have been searched by the NYPD or federal agents. When police come through your door with a warrant, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Police Took Your Phone or Computer

When police seize your phone, laptop, or tablet, they generally need a search warrant to look through it (Riley v. California), but they can hold the device while they get one, and they may ask for your passcode or try to unlock it themselves.

It may mean investigators believe your messages, photos, location history, or files contain evidence of a crime, and it can expose years of your private life to prosecutors and leave you without your device for months while it sits in the NYPD Property Clerk’s office.

What you say about the device, including whether you give up your passcode, can be used against you.

Forbes Stranger defends individuals, professionals, students, and business owners whose phones and computers have been seized by the NYPD or federal agents. When police are holding your digital life, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Google or Apple Says Police Requested Your Data

An email from Google, Apple, Meta, Microsoft, or another provider saying law enforcement requested your account data means police or prosecutors served a subpoena, court order, or search warrant for your information under the Stored Communications Act (18 U.S.C. § 2703).

It may mean you are a target, a subject, or a witness in a state or federal investigation, and it can give investigators your emails, messages, photos, location history, and search activity going back years.

The notice may be your only warning, with a short window to challenge the request before your data is turned over.

Forbes Stranger defends individuals, executives, professionals, and students whose Google, Apple, and social media accounts are the subject of law enforcement requests. When the government comes for your digital records, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Searched or Questioned by Customs at JFK

A customs search at JFK is a border search, which allows U.S. Customs and Border Protection officers to question you, search your luggage, and inspect your phone or laptop without a warrant when you enter or leave the country.

It may mean your name, travel, or belongings flagged you for secondary inspection or for an open investigation by Homeland Security Investigations, and it can lead to the seizure of your devices, cash, or goods, arrest at the airport, and consequences for your visa or green card.

What you said to officers, and what they copied from your phone, can surface months later in a federal case.

Forbes Stranger defends travelers, business owners, students, and non-citizens who have been searched, questioned, or detained by customs at JFK and other New York airports. When a trip home turns into an investigation, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Police Want Your DNA or a Lineup

When police ask you for a DNA swab, fingerprints, or to stand in a lineup, they are asking you to provide identification evidence, and without your consent they generally need a court order to take your DNA (Matter of Abe A.).

It may mean detectives believe your DNA or your appearance will connect you to a crime scene, a victim, or a witness’s description, and it can produce the single piece of evidence that turns an open investigation into an arrest.

Agreeing on the spot can waive challenges you would otherwise have to how the evidence is collected.

Forbes Stranger defends individuals, students, and professionals asked by the NYPD or federal agents to provide DNA, fingerprints, or appear in a lineup. When police want your DNA, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Asked to Take a Lie Detector Test

A police request to take a lie detector test, or polygraph, is an invitation to a recorded interrogation, and while polygraph results are not admissible as evidence in New York courts (People v. Shedrick), everything you say before, during, and after the test can be.

It may mean detectives consider you a suspect and are using the test to pressure you, and it can end with officers telling you that you failed and pushing for a confession that becomes the center of the case against you.

Agreeing to prove your innocence this way often hands detectives the statement they were looking for.

Forbes Stranger defends individuals, students, professionals, and parents who have been asked to take a polygraph by the NYPD or federal agents. When police want to wire you to a machine, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Preserving Video and Evidence That Can Clear You

Preserving evidence means locking down the video, phone records, messages, and witness accounts that show where you were and what really happened, through preservation letters, subpoenas, and an independent defense investigation, often before the prosecution’s discovery obligations (Criminal Procedure Law Article 245) even begin.

It may mean the evidence that clears you is sitting on a store’s camera system, a building’s hard drive, or a witness’s phone, and it can be erased within days, leaving only the version of events the police chose to collect.

Much of the surveillance video in New York City is recorded over in a matter of days or weeks.

Forbes Stranger defends individuals, families, and professionals in investigations involving digital evidence and possible loss of important records. When the truth is on a hard drive that is about to be erased, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Subpoenaed to Testify Before a Grand Jury

A grand jury subpoena requires you to appear and testify as a witness before a New York grand jury, and in most cases a witness compelled to give evidence generally receives transactional immunity under CPL § 190.40 absent an effective waiver; statutory exceptions include perjury and false statements in that testimony (Criminal Procedure Law §§ 50.20, 190.40).

It may mean prosecutors believe you saw something, know someone involved, or have information about a shooting, assault, homicide, or other crime, and it can expose you to perjury charges, contempt for refusing to appear, and pressure to sign away your immunity.

Your lawyer cannot come into the grand jury room unless you waive immunity, so every decision has to be made before you walk in.

Forbes Stranger defends witnesses, family members, bystanders, and friends of the accused who have been subpoenaed to testify before grand juries in all five boroughs. When a grand jury summons you, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Secret (Sealed) Indictments

A sealed indictment is a set of criminal charges a grand jury has voted on in secret and kept hidden from the public, and from you, until you are arrested, under New York’s grand jury secrecy rules (Criminal Procedure Law § 190.25).

It may mean prosecutors have already charged you with a felony and are waiting to make an arrest, often in a coordinated early-morning sweep, and it can mean you learn about the case only when officers arrive at your door.

A lawyer may be able to confirm the indictment and arrange a surrender before police arrest you in front of your family or coworkers.

Forbes Stranger defends individuals, executives, professionals, and families facing sealed state and federal indictments in New York. When the charges are already written but kept from you, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Held in Jail Waiting for Indictment (180.80 Day)

Under New York’s 180.80 rule (Criminal Procedure Law § 180.80), a person held in jail on a felony complaint may seek release on recognizance after 120 hours, or 144 hours when a weekend or holiday intervenes, if there has been no qualifying felony-complaint disposition or hearing commencement, subject to the statutory exceptions for consent, indictment vote/certification and good cause.

It may mean you or your loved one is sitting in custody while prosecutors race to present the case to a grand jury, and it can be the moment prosecutors push for a quick plea or ask you to waive the deadline, trading away your leverage for more time.

The decisions made before 180.80 day, including whether to testify before the grand jury or waive the deadline, can decide whether you go home.

Forbes Stranger defends people held on felony complaints. When the clock is running on your freedom, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Getting an Indictment Dismissed (CPL § 210.20)

A New York indictment may be dismissed or reduced when the charges are legally defective, the grand jury evidence is insufficient, or another statutory ground applies (CPL § 210.20). The prosecution may seek to resubmit certain charges, and a dismissal does not always end the matter permanently. Forbes Stranger represents individuals facing felony indictments throughout New York City. When an indictment puts your freedom at risk, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Stopping Charges Before They Are Filed

Stopping charges before they are filed means presenting evidence, witnesses, and legal arguments to the police, the District Attorney, or federal prosecutors while the charging decision is still open, and, when charges cannot be avoided, negotiating reduced charges through a waiver of indictment and a Superior Court Information (Criminal Procedure Law Article 195).

It may mean investigators are close to a decision and still weighing whether the evidence supports an arrest or indictment, and it can be the difference between a case that is declined, a reduced charge, and a public felony prosecution.

Once an arrest is made or an indictment is filed, the leverage shifts to the prosecution.

Forbes Stranger defends individuals, executives, professionals, students, and families in state and federal investigations before charges are filed. When the decision to charge you has not been made yet, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Cooperating With Prosecutors

Cooperating with prosecutors means giving information or testimony to the District Attorney or federal prosecutors, usually starting with a proffer session under a written agreement, in exchange for the possibility of reduced charges or a lower sentence, including a sentence below a federal mandatory minimum (18 U.S.C. § 3553(e)).

It may mean prosecutors believe you have valuable information about others, and while it can bring real benefits, statements made in a proffer can be used against you in ways most people do not expect, and cooperation can put your safety and your relationships at risk.

The terms of a proffer or cooperation agreement are set before you say a word and cannot be undone after.

Forbes Stranger defends individuals, executives, and professionals in proffer sessions and cooperation agreements in state and federal cases. When prosecutors want you on their side, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Questioned as a Witness

Being questioned as a witness means police, detectives, or prosecutors want to interview you about a crime they are investigating, and in New York a person can be called a witness one day and treated as a suspect the next.

It may mean investigators think you saw something, know someone involved, or have records or messages they need, and it can lead to statements that expose you to charges, a material witness order requiring you to appear or post bail (Criminal Procedure Law Article 620), or pressure to testify against people you know.

Investigators rarely tell you when you have stopped being a witness and become a suspect.

Forbes Stranger defends witnesses, bystanders, employees, and family members questioned by the NYPD, District Attorneys, and federal agents. When investigators say you are just a witness, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Sued and Investigated at the Same Time

Being sued and investigated at the same time means you are facing a civil lawsuit, such as a wrongful death, assault, fraud, or employment case, while police or prosecutors investigate the same events, and courts can be asked to pause the civil case to protect you (CPLR § 2201).

It may mean the person suing you has gone to the police, or that prosecutors are watching the lawsuit for evidence, and it can turn your deposition, documents, and sworn answers into the prosecution’s case, while staying silent in the civil case can be held against you.

Every word in the civil case must be coordinated with your criminal defense before it is spoken.

Forbes Stranger defends individuals, business owners, executives, and professionals facing parallel civil lawsuits and criminal investigations. When one set of facts puts your money and your freedom on the line, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Your Name in the News

Having your name in the news during a criminal investigation means reporters, prosecutors’ press releases, or social media are connecting you to an alleged crime, often before any charge is filed or proven, and New York’s ethics rules limit what lawyers on both sides can say publicly (Rule of Professional Conduct 3.6).

It may mean your case is drawing attention because of who you are, where you work, or what you are accused of, and it can cost you your job, your business relationships, and your reputation long before a court decides anything.

A single public statement from you, your company, or your family can become evidence or a headline.

Forbes Stranger defends executives, public figures, professionals, and families in investigations that draw media attention. When your case plays out in public, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.
