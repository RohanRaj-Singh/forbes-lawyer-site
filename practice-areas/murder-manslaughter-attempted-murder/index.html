Murder, Manslaughter & Attempted Murder

Murder 2nd Degree (PL § 125.25)

Murder in the second degree (Penal Law § 125.25) is the most common murder charge in New York, and it accuses someone of intentionally causing another person’s death, causing a death through conduct showing depraved indifference to human life, or causing a death during a robbery, burglary, or other serious felony. It may mean New York prosecutors believe a shooting, stabbing, fight, or robbery that ended in a death was an intentional killing, often based on surveillance video, cell phone records, forensic evidence, or the word of a cooperating witness, and it carries a sentence of 15 to 25 years to life in state prison. Forbes Stranger defends individuals, young adults, and professionals in Murder 2nd degree prosecutions in New York State Supreme Court in every borough. When prosecutors are seeking a life sentence, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Murder 1st Degree (PL § 125.27)

Murder in the first degree (Penal Law § 125.27) is New York’s most serious murder charge, and it accuses someone age 18 or older of an intentional killing with an aggravating factor, such as killing a police officer or a witness, killing more than one person, a murder for hire, torture, or an intentional killing during a robbery, burglary, kidnapping, or other serious felony. It may mean New York prosecutors believe the circumstances of a death put it in the worst category the law recognizes, and it can carry life in prison without parole, or a sentence of 20 to 25 years to life. Forbes Stranger defends individuals in Murder 1st degree prosecutions in New York State Supreme Court in every borough. When prosecutors are seeking life without parole, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Aggravated Murder (PL § 125.26)

Aggravated murder (Penal Law § 125.26) is a New York charge that accuses someone older than 18 of intentionally killing an on-duty police officer, peace officer, firefighter, or emergency medical worker, or of intentionally killing a child under 14 after torturing them in an especially cruel manner. It may mean New York prosecutors believe a confrontation with law enforcement or first responders, or the death of a child, was intentional and especially cruel, and a conviction under § 125.26(1) requires life without parole; under § 125.26(2), the court may impose life without parole or another authorized class A-I felony sentence. Forbes Stranger defends individuals in aggravated murder prosecutions in New York State Supreme Court in every borough. When prosecutors are seeking a sentence that never ends, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Felony Murder (PL § 125.25(3))

Felony murder (Penal Law § 125.25(3)) is a form of murder in the second degree that accuses someone of taking part in a robbery, burglary, kidnapping, arson, or other serious felony during which anyone involved caused the death of a non-participant, even without proof of an intent to kill; non-killing participants may have the specific four-part affirmative defense in § 125.25(3). It may mean New York prosecutors are holding you responsible for a death caused by someone else in your group during a robbery or burglary, or even by a heart attack brought on by the crime, and it carries the same sentence as an intentional killing: 15 to 25 years to life in state prison. Forbes Stranger defends young adults and individuals charged with felony murder in every borough, including through the statutory defense available to participants who were unarmed and had no reason to expect violence. When prosecutors charge you with a killing you did not commit, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Depraved Indifference Murder (PL § 125.25(2))

Depraved indifference murder (Penal Law § 125.25(2)) is a form of murder in the second degree that accuses someone of causing a death through reckless conduct so extreme that it shows utter disregard for human life, even without any intent to kill, such as firing into a crowd, driving onto a crowded sidewalk, or playing Russian roulette. It may mean New York prosecutors are calling a reckless act murder rather than manslaughter, a line New York’s highest court has drawn narrowly (People v. Feingold), and it carries the same sentence as an intentional killing: 15 to 25 years to life in state prison. Forbes Stranger defends drivers, young adults, and individuals in depraved indifference murder prosecutions in every borough. When a reckless moment is charged as murder, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Attempted Murder 2nd Degree (PL §§ 110.00, 125.25)

Attempted murder in the second degree (Penal Law §§ 110.00, 125.25) is a New York violent felony that accuses someone of trying to kill another person with the intent to cause their death, whether or not anyone was actually injured. It may mean New York prosecutors believe a shooting, stabbing, or attack was meant to kill rather than to injure or scare, often charged alongside Assault 1st and weapons possession, and it can carry a mandatory minimum of five years and up to 25 years in state prison, plus up to five years of post-release supervision. Forbes Stranger defends young adults and individuals in attempted murder prosecutions in New York State Supreme Court in every borough. When prosecutors claim you meant to take a life, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Attempted Murder of a Police Officer (Attempted Murder 1st Degree, PL §§ 110.00, 125.27)

Attempted murder of a police officer is charged in New York as attempted murder in the first degree (Penal Law §§ 110.00, 125.27) or attempted aggravated murder (Penal Law §§ 110.00, 125.26). It accuses someone of trying to kill an on-duty police officer, peace officer, or other first responder, or of an attempted killing with another aggravating factor. It may mean New York prosecutors believe shots fired during a police chase, a stop, or a raid were aimed at officers, and it carries a sentence of up to life in prison, with 20 to 40 years served before any chance of parole. Forbes Stranger defends individuals charged with attempting to kill police officers in New York State Supreme Court in every borough. When the police are both the accusers and the witnesses, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Conspiracy to Commit Murder and Murder-for-Hire Charges (PL §§ 100.10-100.13, 105.15, 105.17)

Conspiracy to commit murder can be charged as second-degree conspiracy (Penal Law § 105.15) where the defendant agrees with one or more persons to commit a class A felony. First-degree conspiracy (§ 105.17) additionally requires that a defendant older than 18 agree with a person younger than 16 to commit a class A felony; an overt act is required under § 105.20. Criminal solicitation (Penal Law §§ 100.10, 100.13) accuses someone of asking, paying, or urging another person to commit a murder, the state-court version of a murder-for-hire case. It may mean New York prosecutors believe a conversation, a payment, or messages with an informant or an undercover officer posing as a hitman were a real plan to kill, even though no one was harmed, and it can carry up to 25 years in state prison for conspiracy in the second degree, and substantially greater exposure on more serious charges. Forbes Stranger defends business owners, spouses, professionals, and individuals in murder conspiracy and murder-for-hire prosecutions in every borough. When prosecutors say you planned a murder that never happened, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Manslaughter 1st Degree (PL § 125.20)

Manslaughter in the first degree (Penal Law § 125.20) is a New York violent felony that accuses someone of causing a death while intending to cause serious physical injury, or of intentionally killing someone while acting under an extreme emotional disturbance. It may mean New York prosecutors believe a fight, a beating, a stabbing, or a single punch that ended in a death was meant to seriously hurt but not to kill, and it can carry a mandatory minimum of five years and up to 25 years in state prison, plus up to five years of post-release supervision. Forbes Stranger defends young adults, workers, and individuals in Manslaughter 1st degree prosecutions in New York State Supreme Court in every borough. When a fight ends in a death and prosecutors want decades, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Manslaughter 2nd Degree (PL § 125.15)

Manslaughter in the second degree (Penal Law § 125.15) is a New York felony that accuses someone of recklessly causing another person’s death, meaning they were aware of a substantial and unjustifiable risk that their conduct could kill someone and consciously disregarded it, or of intentionally helping someone end their own life. It may mean New York prosecutors believe an accidental shooting, a dangerous act at a construction site, a fatal drug sale, a crash, or a fight that went too far was reckless rather than accidental, and it can carry up to 15 years in state prison. Forbes Stranger defends drivers, contractors, parents, young adults, and individuals in Manslaughter 2nd degree prosecutions in every borough. When an accident is charged as a homicide, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Criminally Negligent Homicide (PL § 125.10)

Criminally negligent homicide (Penal Law § 125.10) is a New York felony that accuses someone of causing a death by failing to perceive a substantial and unjustifiable risk, in a way that was a gross departure from how a reasonable person would have acted, even with no intent to harm anyone. It may mean New York prosecutors believe a car crash, an unsafe job site, a building violation, a child left unattended, or another lapse in care caused a death, and it can carry up to four years in state prison, a felony record, and civil lawsuits from the family based on the same facts. Forbes Stranger defends drivers, contractors, landlords, parents, professionals, and business owners in criminally negligent homicide prosecutions in every borough. When a tragedy is charged as a crime, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Aggravated Manslaughter: Death of a Police Officer (PL §§ 125.11, 125.21, 125.22)

Aggravated manslaughter (Penal Law §§ 125.21, 125.22) and aggravated criminally negligent homicide (Penal Law § 125.11) are New York felonies that accuse someone of causing the death of an on-duty police officer or peace officer, through intent to cause serious injury, reckless conduct, or criminal negligence, without an intent to kill. It may mean New York prosecutors believe a car chase, a struggle during an arrest, or a crash involving a police vehicle caused an officer’s death, and it can carry up to 15 years in state prison for aggravated criminally negligent homicide or aggravated manslaughter in the second degree, and up to 25 years for aggravated manslaughter in the first degree. Forbes Stranger defends drivers and individuals in aggravated manslaughter prosecutions in New York State Supreme Court in every borough. When the death of an officer puts the full weight of the system against you, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Charged After a Fatal Overdose

A charge after a fatal overdose accuses someone of causing a death by selling, giving, or sharing drugs, often fentanyl or pills laced with fentanyl. New York has no separate drug-induced homicide law, so prosecutors charge manslaughter in the second degree (Penal Law § 125.15), criminally negligent homicide (Penal Law § 125.10), and criminal sale of a controlled substance (Penal Law Article 220). It may mean New York prosecutors traced a friend’s, partner’s, or customer’s overdose back to you through text messages, phone records, or payment apps, and it can carry up to 15 years in state prison, while federal prosecutors can bring distribution-resulting-in-death charges with a 20-year mandatory minimum. Forbes Stranger defends friends, partners, family members, and individuals charged after fatal overdoses in state and federal court. When a loss is turned into a homicide case, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Accused in a Child’s Death (Shaken Baby, Abuse, and Neglect Cases)

An accusation in a child’s death can bring New York charges ranging from murder in the second degree for depraved indifference to a child under 11 (Penal Law § 125.25(4)) and manslaughter in the first degree (Penal Law § 125.20(4)) in alleged abuse and shaken baby cases, to manslaughter in the second degree and criminally negligent homicide in neglect cases, such as a child left in a car, unsupervised near water, or in an unsafe sleeping position. It may mean New York prosecutors and medical examiners believe an infant’s head injury, a fall, or a sudden death was caused by shaking, abuse, or neglect, and it can carry up to life in state prison, along with ACS removal of your other children and a Family Court case. Forbes Stranger defends parents, grandparents, babysitters, nannies, and daycare workers accused in a child’s death in every borough. When you are grieving and accused at the same time, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Contractor or Employer Charged After a Worker’s Death

A contractor or employer charged after a worker’s death can face New York charges of manslaughter in the second degree (Penal Law § 125.15), criminally negligent homicide (Penal Law § 125.10), and reckless endangerment. These charges are brought against individual owners, site supervisors, and foremen, and against the company itself (Penal Law § 20.20). It may mean prosecutors, often working with the Department of Buildings and OSHA, believe missing fall protection, an unsafe excavation, ignored violations, or untrained workers caused a fatal accident, and it can carry up to 15 years in state prison for individuals and, under Carlos’ Law, corporate fines reaching hundreds of thousands of dollars or more, along with debarment from public contracts. Forbes Stranger defends construction company owners, general contractors, subcontractors, site safety managers, and employers in workplace death prosecutions and investigations in every borough. When a jobsite accident becomes a homicide case, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Vehicular Manslaughter 2nd Degree (PL § 125.12)

Vehicular manslaughter in the second degree (Penal Law § 125.12) is a New York felony that accuses someone of causing another person’s death while operating in violation of specified intoxication or impairment provisions, when the intoxication or impairment results in operation in a manner causing the death; the statute supplies a rebuttable causation presumption under specified circumstances. It may mean New York prosecutors believe alcohol, cannabis, or another drug played a role in a fatal crash, often based on a blood test, a breath test, or a drug recognition evaluation taken hours later, and it can carry up to seven years in state prison and the revocation of your driver’s license. Forbes Stranger defends drivers, commercial drivers, rideshare drivers, and individuals in vehicular manslaughter prosecutions in every borough. When a crash ends a life and prosecutors blame you, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Vehicular Manslaughter 1st Degree (PL § 125.13)

Vehicular manslaughter in the first degree (Penal Law § 125.13) is a New York felony that accuses someone of causing a death while driving drunk or impaired with an aggravating factor, such as a blood alcohol content of .18 or higher, driving with a suspended or revoked license, a prior DWI conviction within ten years, causing more than one death, or having a child 15 or younger in the car. It may mean New York prosecutors believe your record, your license status, or the circumstances of a fatal crash make it the most serious form of vehicular manslaughter, and it can carry up to 15 years in state prison and the long-term or permanent loss of your driving privileges. Forbes Stranger defends drivers, commercial drivers, and individuals in vehicular manslaughter in the first degree prosecutions in every borough. When prosecutors use your past to raise the stakes after a fatal crash, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Aggravated Vehicular Homicide (PL § 125.14)

Aggravated vehicular homicide (Penal Law § 125.14) is New York’s most serious vehicular homicide charge, and it accuses someone of driving recklessly while drunk or impaired and causing a death, with an aggravating factor such as a blood alcohol content of .18 or higher, a suspended or revoked license, a prior DWI, the death of more than one person, or a death and a serious injury in the same crash. It may mean New York prosecutors believe speeding, running lights, or racing while intoxicated caused a fatal crash, and it can carry up to 25 years in state prison and the revocation of your driver’s license. Forbes Stranger defends drivers and individuals in aggravated vehicular homicide prosecutions in New York State Supreme Court in every borough. When a single crash puts decades of your life on the line, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Fatal Hit-and-Run Charges (VTL § 600(2))

A fatal hit-and-run, charged as leaving the scene of an incident resulting in death (Vehicle and Traffic Law § 600(2)), is a New York felony that accuses a driver of leaving the scene of a crash that killed someone, knowing or having reason to know a person was hurt, without stopping, identifying themselves, and reporting to police. It may mean investigators used traffic cameras, license plate readers, surveillance video, or damage to your car to connect you to a crash you may not have realized involved a person, and it can carry up to seven years in state prison, the revocation of your license, and added homicide charges if prosecutors claim you were impaired or reckless. Forbes Stranger defends drivers, rideshare and delivery drivers, commercial drivers, and individuals in fatal hit-and-run prosecutions in every borough. When police come to your door days after a crash, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Loved One Arrested for Murder

When someone you love is arrested for murder in New York City, they are held through Central Booking and arraigned on a felony complaint. The court can consider release, bail, or remand under New York’s securing-order laws (Criminal Procedure Law § 530.20). It may mean your family member is facing remand to Rikers Island while prosecutors present the case to a grand jury within days, and it can also mean that what they say on jail phone calls, and what you say to detectives or on social media, becomes evidence. Forbes Stranger represents people arrested for murder and guides their families from the first hours at the precinct through arraignment, grand jury, bail applications, and trial in every borough. When your family is facing the worst day of its life, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between them and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Homicide Detectives Want to Talk to You

A call or visit from NYPD homicide detectives means you are connected to a death investigation, whether as a witness, someone who knew the victim, or a suspect, and detectives often describe everyone as “just a witness” until they are ready to make an arrest. It may mean detectives already have video, phone records, or a statement that places you near the scene, and anything you say can become the evidence used to charge you with murder, recorded on video under New York’s interrogation recording law (Criminal Procedure Law § 60.45). Forbes Stranger represents witnesses, family members, friends, and individuals contacted by homicide detectives in every borough, and once your lawyer is involved, New York’s strong right to counsel bars police from questioning you without your attorney present. When homicide detectives want a conversation, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Charged With Attempted Murder After a Shooting

A shooting arrest in New York usually brings a stack of charges: attempted murder in the second degree (Penal Law §§ 110.00, 125.25), assault in the first degree (Penal Law § 120.10), and criminal possession of a weapon in the second degree (Penal Law § 265.03), whether or not anyone was hit. It may mean New York prosecutors are relying on ShotSpotter alerts, surveillance video, shell casing matches, cell phone location data, social media posts, or an eyewitness identification to put the gun in your hand, and it can carry up to 25 years in state prison on the attempted murder count alone, with weapons charges that carry their own mandatory minimums. Forbes Stranger defends young adults and individuals in shooting and attempted murder prosecutions in New York State Supreme Court in every borough. When prosecutors say you pulled the trigger, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Charged With Murder but You Weren’t the Shooter (Acting in Concert, PL § 20.00)

Being charged with murder when you were not the shooter usually means prosecutors are using New York’s accomplice liability law, called acting in concert (Penal Law § 20.00). Accomplice liability and felony murder are distinct theories with different legal requirements; the mere fact that someone was present or associated with others does not itself establish guilt. It may mean New York prosecutors believe you were part of a plan because you were in the car, in the group, or on the phone before or after a shooting, and it carries the same sentence as the person who fired the shots: 15 to 25 years to life in state prison for murder in the second degree. Forbes Stranger defends drivers, friends, family members, and young adults charged with murder as accomplices in every borough. When prosecutors charge you for what someone else did, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Charged With Helping After a Homicide (Hindering Prosecution and Evidence Tampering, PL §§ 205.65, 215.40)

Hindering prosecution (Penal Law §§ 205.60, 205.65) and tampering with physical evidence (Penal Law § 215.40) are New York felonies that accuse someone of helping a person after a homicide, such as hiding them, giving them money, a ride, or a place to stay, warning them about police, or getting rid of a gun, clothing, a phone, or video. It may mean New York prosecutors believe you helped a friend, partner, or family member after a shooting or killing, even if you had nothing to do with the death itself, and New York law makes no exception for family. It can carry up to seven years in state prison for hindering prosecution in the first degree and up to four years for evidence tampering. Forbes Stranger defends parents, partners, siblings, friends, and individuals charged with helping after a homicide in every borough. When loyalty to someone you love becomes a felony charge, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Arrested in a Cold Case

A cold case arrest is a murder or other serious charge brought years or even decades after the crime, which New York allows because there is no statute of limitations for murder and other class A felonies (Criminal Procedure Law § 30.10). It may mean New York prosecutors have reopened an old case with new DNA testing, genetic genealogy, a cooperating witness, or reinterpreted evidence, and it can carry the same life sentence as a case charged the day after the crime, even though the evidence that could prove your innocence may be gone. Forbes Stranger defends individuals arrested in cold case homicides in every borough. When the past comes back as a murder charge, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Teen Charged With Murder (PL § 30.00; CPL Article 722)

When a teenager is charged with murder in New York, children as young as 13 can be prosecuted as juvenile offenders (Penal Law § 30.00), and 16- and 17-year-olds are charged as adolescent offenders, with both cases heard in the Youth Part of Supreme Court (Criminal Procedure Law Article 722) rather than Family Court. It may mean your son or daughter is facing an adult-style murder prosecution for a shooting, a fight, or a robbery that ended in a death, often alongside older co-defendants, and it can carry a sentence of up to life in state prison, while youthful offender treatment is generally not available for a murder conviction. Forbes Stranger defends teenagers and young adults charged with murder in the Youth Part and Supreme Court in every borough. When your child’s entire future is on the line, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between them and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Killed Someone in Self-Defense (PL § 35.15)

Killing someone in self-defense is legally justified in New York (Penal Law § 35.15) when you reasonably believed the other person was using or about to use deadly force against you or someone else, or was committing a robbery, kidnapping, rape, or home burglary (Penal Law § 35.20). Outside your home, you generally must retreat first if you can do so in complete safety. It may mean police arrested you because you were the one left standing, and it can lead to murder or manslaughter charges unless your lawyer builds the justification defense early, because once it is raised, prosecutors must disprove it beyond a reasonable doubt under a test that weighs what you believed and whether a reasonable person in your situation would have believed it too (People v. Goetz). Forbes Stranger defends homeowners, business owners, workers, and individuals who used deadly force to protect themselves or others, starting at the grand jury, where justification must be presented and charged. When saving your own life leads to a homicide charge, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Insanity Defense and Extreme Emotional Disturbance (PL §§ 40.15, 125.25(1)(a))

The insanity defense in New York (Penal Law § 40.15) is an affirmative defense that a person, because of mental illness or defect, lacked substantial capacity to know or appreciate the nature of their conduct or that it was wrong. Extreme emotional disturbance (Penal Law § 125.25(1)(a)) can reduce an intentional killing from murder to manslaughter when it was driven by an emotional state with a reasonable explanation. It may mean a loved one with a history of mental illness, psychosis, or trauma is facing a murder charge, and it can lead to commitment to a secure psychiatric facility instead of prison, a manslaughter sentence instead of life, or a hearing on whether they are even fit to stand trial (Criminal Procedure Law Article 730), with strict notice deadlines for raising these defenses (Criminal Procedure Law § 250.10). Forbes Stranger represents individuals and families in homicide cases involving mental illness and extreme emotional disturbance in every borough. When a mental health crisis ends in a homicide charge, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Murder Trial Defense

A murder trial in New York is held in Supreme Court before a jury of 12, all of whom must agree before anyone can be convicted. It is preceded by pretrial hearings that can suppress statements, searches, and identifications (Huntley, Mapp, and Wade hearings), and by the broad discovery New York now requires prosecutors to turn over (Criminal Procedure Law Article 245). It may mean prosecutors will present medical examiner findings, ballistics, DNA, cell phone location data, surveillance video, and cooperating witnesses, and it can end in a sentence of up to life in state prison if the defense is not prepared to take apart every piece of that case in front of a jury. Forbes Stranger defends individuals at murder trials in New York State Supreme Court in every borough. When your life will be decided by a jury, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.
