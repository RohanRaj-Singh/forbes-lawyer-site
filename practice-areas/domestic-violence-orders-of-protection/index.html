Domestic Violence & Orders of Protection

Arrested for Domestic Violence (CPL § 140.10(4))

A domestic violence arrest is an arrest for an alleged offense against a spouse, partner, ex, family member, or someone you share a child with. New York’s mandatory-arrest provisions (Criminal Procedure Law § 140.10(4)) require arrest in specified qualifying family-offense, felony, and protection-order circumstances when the statutory requirements are met; not every domestic-incident call requires an arrest. It may mean New York prosecutors are treating a single argument, 911 call, or Domestic Incident Report as a criminal case, and it can lead to a night in Central Booking, an order of protection that bars you from your home and children, and charges ranging from harassment to felony assault. Forbes Stranger defends spouses, partners, parents, professionals, and family members in domestic violence prosecutions in every borough’s criminal court. When one argument puts your home, your family, and your freedom on the line, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Assault 3rd Degree Domestic Violence (PL § 120.00)

Assault in the third degree (Penal Law § 120.00) is a New York crime that accuses someone of causing physical injury, meaning pain or impairment, to another person, and it is the most common charge filed after a domestic incident involving a spouse, partner, or family member. It may mean New York prosecutors believe a push, slap, grab, or struggle during an argument caused pain or a visible mark, and it can carry up to 364 days in jail, an order of protection lasting up to five years after the sentence, and a potential federal firearms prohibition if the conviction meets the federal definition of a misdemeanor crime of domestic violence (18 U.S.C. §§ 921(a)(33), 922(g)(9)). Forbes Stranger defends spouses, partners, parents, professionals, and family members in Assault 3rd degree domestic violence prosecutions in every borough’s criminal court. When an argument at home becomes an assault case, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Assault 2nd Degree Domestic Violence (PL § 120.05)

Assault in the second degree (Penal Law § 120.05) is a New York violent felony that accuses someone of causing serious physical injury, or of causing injury with a weapon or a “dangerous instrument,” which can include an everyday object like a phone, a bottle, a belt, or a kitchen utensil, as well as injuring someone 65 or older when the accused is more than ten years younger. It may mean New York prosecutors believe an argument at home involved a broken bone, a cut, an object thrown or swung during a fight, or an injury to an elderly parent, and it can carry up to seven years in state prison, a violent felony record, and an order of protection lasting up to eight years after the sentence. Forbes Stranger defends spouses, partners, parents, adult children, and professionals in Assault 2nd degree and felony domestic assault prosecutions in every borough. When a fight at home is charged as a violent felony, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Assault 1st Degree Domestic Violence (PL § 120.10)

Assault in the first degree (Penal Law § 120.10) is one of New York’s most serious violent felonies, and it accuses someone of intentionally causing serious physical injury with a deadly weapon or dangerous instrument, intentionally causing serious and permanent disfigurement, or causing serious injury through conduct showing depraved indifference to human life. It may mean New York prosecutors believe a domestic incident involved a stabbing, a burn, a thrown liquid, or a beating that caused lasting harm, and it can carry a mandatory minimum of five years and up to 25 years in state prison, often charged alongside attempted murder. Forbes Stranger defends spouses, partners, parents, and family members in Assault 1st degree domestic violence prosecutions in every borough. When prosecutors are seeking decades in prison over a single night at home, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Criminal Obstruction of Breathing: Choking Charges (PL § 121.11)

Criminal obstruction of breathing or blood circulation (Penal Law § 121.11) is a New York crime that accuses someone of putting pressure on another person’s neck or throat, or blocking their nose or mouth, with intent to interfere with their breathing or blood flow. It may mean New York prosecutors believe a hand on the neck, a hold, or a struggle during an argument was an attempt to choke, even when there are no visible marks, and it can carry up to 364 days in jail, a full order of protection, and a domestic violence record that prosecutors use to seek felony charges if anything happens again. Forbes Stranger defends spouses, partners, parents, and family members in criminal obstruction of breathing prosecutions in every borough’s criminal court. When a moment of contact is charged as choking, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Strangulation 2nd Degree (PL § 121.12)

Strangulation in the second degree (Penal Law § 121.12) is a New York violent felony that accuses someone of obstructing another person’s breathing or blood circulation and causing stupor, a loss of consciousness for any length of time, or any other physical injury or impairment. It may mean New York prosecutors believe the other person blacked out, felt dizzy, or had pain or marks after a struggle at home, and it can carry up to seven years in state prison, a violent felony record, and an order of protection lasting up to eight years after the sentence. Forbes Stranger defends spouses, partners, parents, and family members in strangulation in the second degree prosecutions in every borough. When an accusation of choking becomes a violent felony, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Strangulation 1st Degree (PL § 121.13)

Strangulation in the first degree (Penal Law § 121.13) is New York’s most serious strangulation charge, and it accuses someone of obstructing another person’s breathing or blood circulation and causing serious physical injury. It may mean New York prosecutors believe a domestic incident left the other person with lasting harm, such as a serious throat or brain injury, and it can carry a mandatory minimum of three and a half years and up to 15 years in state prison. Forbes Stranger defends spouses, partners, parents, and family members in strangulation in the first degree prosecutions in every borough. When prosecutors are seeking a long prison sentence over a single night at home, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Aggravated Harassment 2nd Degree: Calls, Texts, and Social Media (PL § 240.30)

Aggravated harassment in the second degree (Penal Law § 240.30) is a New York crime that accuses someone of threatening another person by phone, text, email, social media, or other electronic communication, knowing the message will cause fear of physical harm or property damage, or of making repeated calls with no legitimate purpose. It may mean New York prosecutors believe angry texts, voicemails, DMs, or calls during a breakup or family dispute crossed the line into threats, and it can carry up to 364 days in jail, an order of protection that bars all contact, including by phone and social media, and a domestic violence record. Forbes Stranger defends spouses, partners, exes, parents, and family members in aggravated harassment prosecutions in every borough’s criminal court. When your messages are turned into a criminal case, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Harassment 2nd Degree in a Relationship (PL § 240.26)

Harassment in the second degree (Penal Law § 240.26) is a New York offense that accuses someone of striking, shoving, kicking, or otherwise making physical contact with another person, or threatening to, with intent to harass, annoy, or alarm them, and it is often charged in domestic incidents alongside or instead of assault. It may mean New York prosecutors believe a push, a grab, or a heated moment during an argument was meant to harass, and while it is a violation rather than a crime, it can carry up to 15 days in jail and an order of protection that keeps you away from your home and family for up to two years. Forbes Stranger defends spouses, partners, exes, parents, and family members in harassment 2nd degree cases in every borough’s criminal court. When prosecutors treat an argument as harassment, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Menacing in a Relationship (PL §§ 120.13-120.15)

Menacing (Penal Law §§ 120.13-120.15) is a New York crime that accuses someone of intentionally placing another person in fear of physical injury or death through physical menace, by displaying a weapon or dangerous instrument, through a repeated pattern of conduct, or in violation of an order of protection. It may mean New York prosecutors believe raising a hand, holding a knife or other object during an argument, or a series of confrontations put a partner or family member in fear, and it can carry up to three months in jail for menacing in the third degree, up to 364 days for the second degree, and up to four years in state prison for menacing in the first degree, a felony charged when there is a prior conviction. Forbes Stranger defends spouses, partners, exes, parents, and family members in menacing prosecutions in every borough’s criminal court. When a heated moment is charged as a threat of violence, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Stalking Charges (PL §§ 120.45-120.60)

Stalking (Penal Law §§ 120.45-120.60) is a New York crime that accuses someone of a repeated course of conduct aimed at a specific person, such as following, calling, texting, showing up at their home or job, or tracking them through GPS or apps, that causes reasonable fear for their safety, their job, or their emotional well-being, often after being told to stop. It may mean New York prosecutors believe your attempts to reach an ex, a partner, or a family member after a breakup or separation became threatening, and it can carry up to three months in jail for stalking in the fourth degree, up to 364 days for the third degree, and up to seven years in state prison for stalking in the first degree. Forbes Stranger defends spouses, partners, exes, parents, and family members in stalking prosecutions in every borough. When reaching out to someone is recast as stalking, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Unlawful Imprisonment and Coercion Charges (PL §§ 135.05, 135.10, 135.60-135.65)

Unlawful imprisonment (Penal Law §§ 135.05, 135.10) is a New York crime that accuses someone of restraining another person, such as blocking a door, holding them in a room, or preventing them from leaving. Coercion (Penal Law §§ 135.60-135.65) accuses someone of using threats, such as threats of violence, exposure, or immigration consequences, to force a person to do something or to stop them from acting. It may mean New York prosecutors believe you kept a partner or family member from leaving during an argument, took their phone to stop a call for help, or used threats to control what they did, and it can carry up to 364 days in jail for the misdemeanor charges, up to four years in state prison for unlawful imprisonment in the first degree, and up to seven years for coercion in the first degree. Forbes Stranger defends spouses, partners, exes, parents, and family members in unlawful imprisonment and coercion prosecutions in every borough. When standing in a doorway during an argument becomes a criminal charge, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Criminal Mischief: Breaking a Phone or Property During an Argument (PL §§ 145.00-145.10)

Criminal mischief (Penal Law §§ 145.00-145.10) is a New York crime that accuses someone of intentionally damaging another person’s property, and in domestic cases it is often charged when a phone is broken or taken to stop someone from calling 911 (Penal Law § 145.00(4)). It may mean New York prosecutors believe you smashed a phone, a TV, a door, or other property during an argument, even property you paid for or own jointly with a spouse or partner, and it can carry up to 364 days in jail for criminal mischief in the fourth degree, up to four years in state prison when the damage exceeds $250, and up to seven years when it exceeds $1,500. Forbes Stranger defends spouses, partners, exes, parents, and family members in criminal mischief prosecutions in every borough’s criminal court. When a broken phone becomes a criminal case, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Aggravated Family Offense: Repeat Domestic Violence Charges (PL § 240.75)

Aggravated family offense (Penal Law § 240.75) is a New York felony that accuses someone of committing a domestic violence offense, such as assault, menacing, aggravated harassment, stalking, criminal mischief, or criminal obstruction of breathing, against a family or household member after a prior conviction for one of those offenses within the past five years. It may mean New York prosecutors are using an old conviction to turn a new misdemeanor arrest into a felony, and it can carry up to four years in state prison, a felony record, and an order of protection lasting up to eight years after the sentence. Forbes Stranger defends spouses, partners, exes, parents, and family members with prior records in aggravated family offense prosecutions in every borough. When your past is used to turn a misdemeanor into a felony, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Arrested for a Fight With a Parent, Sibling, or Adult Child

A fight with a parent, sibling, adult child, or in-law is treated as domestic violence in New York, because the law covers anyone related by blood or marriage (Criminal Procedure Law § 530.11; Family Court Act § 812). It may mean New York prosecutors are treating an argument at a family home as a domestic violence case, with a mandatory arrest, a full order of protection that bars you from the home you share, and charges that can rise to a felony when the other person is 65 or older (Penal Law § 120.05(12)). Forbes Stranger defends adult children, parents, siblings, and relatives in family-member domestic violence prosecutions in every borough’s criminal court. When a family argument ends in handcuffs, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Falsely Accused of Domestic Violence

Being falsely accused of domestic violence means a spouse, partner, ex, or family member has told police you assaulted, threatened, or harassed them when you did not, and under New York’s mandatory arrest law (Criminal Procedure Law § 140.10(4)), police often make an arrest based on one person’s account at the scene. It may mean New York prosecutors are moving forward on a 911 call or a Domestic Incident Report made in anger, after a breakup, or to get you out of a shared home, and it can lead to an order of protection that removes you from your apartment and children, charges ranging from harassment to felony assault, and a record that follows you long after the truth comes out. Forbes Stranger defends spouses, partners, parents, professionals, and family members falsely accused of domestic violence in every borough. When a false accusation costs you your home and your family overnight, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Domestic Violence Accusations in a Divorce or Custody Case (Domestic Relations Law § 240)

A domestic violence accusation in a divorce or custody case is an allegation of abuse made while a marriage is ending or a custody fight is underway, and New York courts must weigh proven domestic violence when deciding custody and visitation (Domestic Relations Law § 240(1)(a)). It may mean your spouse or co-parent has called the police, filed for an order of protection, or raised abuse claims in Family Court or Supreme Court, and it can lead to criminal charges, removal from the marital home, supervised or suspended visits with your children, and a weaker position in every custody decision that follows. Forbes Stranger defends spouses, parents, and co-parents facing domestic violence accusations during divorce and custody cases. When abuse claims become a weapon in your divorce, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Can the Victim Drop Domestic Violence Charges?

In New York, domestic violence charges are brought by the District Attorney, not the alleged victim, so the person who called the police cannot drop the case on their own, even by signing an affidavit asking prosecutors not to proceed. It may mean prosecutors will try to move forward without the complaining witness, using 911 calls, body camera footage, photos, and statements made at the scene, and contacting that person yourself to ask them to drop it can bring new charges for violating an order of protection (Penal Law § 215.50) or tampering with a witness (Penal Law § 215.10). Forbes Stranger defends spouses, partners, parents, and family members in domestic violence prosecutions where the complaining witness wants the case dropped. When the person who called the police no longer wants a case, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Temporary Order of Protection (TOP) After an Arrest (CPL § 530.12)

A temporary order of protection (Criminal Procedure Law § 530.12), often called a TOP, is a court order issued at arraignment in nearly every domestic violence case. It can bar you from your home, from contacting the protected person by phone, text, social media, or through others, and from seeing your children while the case is pending. It may mean a judge issued the order within hours of your arrest based only on the police paperwork, and it can stay in place for months, require you to surrender your guns (Criminal Procedure Law § 530.14), and expose you to new criminal charges if you violate it, even if the protected person invites the contact. Forbes Stranger defends spouses, partners, parents, and family members subject to temporary orders of protection in every borough’s criminal court. When a court order controls where you live and who you can see, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Kicked Out of Your Own Home by an Order of Protection

Being kicked out of your own home by an order of protection means a judge issued a full stay-away order barring you from the apartment or house where you live, even if your name is on the lease or the deed, often at arraignment and without hearing your side. It may mean you cannot go home, collect your belongings without a police escort, or see children who live there until the order changes, and it can stay in place for months unless your lawyer demands a prompt hearing to challenge it, known as a Forman hearing (People v. Forman). Forbes Stranger defends spouses, partners, parents, and family members barred from their homes by orders of protection in every borough’s criminal court. When an order of protection locks you out of your own home, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Full vs. Limited (Refrain From) Orders of Protection (CPL § 530.12)

A full order of protection (Criminal Procedure Law § 530.12) bars you from all contact with the protected person and requires you to stay away from their home, job, and school. A limited, or “refrain from,” order lets you keep living together and communicating as long as you do not commit any offense against them, such as harassment, threats, or assault. It may mean prosecutors asked for a full stay-away order at arraignment even though you share a home, children, or a life with the protected person, and it can separate you from your family for the life of the case unless your lawyer persuades the court to convert it to a limited order. Forbes Stranger defends spouses, partners, parents, and family members seeking to convert full orders of protection to limited orders in every borough’s criminal court. When the type of order decides whether you can go home, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Getting an Order of Protection Lifted or Changed (CPL § 530.12)

Getting an order of protection lifted or changed means asking the judge who issued it to vacate it, shorten it, or convert it from a full stay-away order to a limited order, because only the court can change it, not the protected person, the police, or the prosecutor (Criminal Procedure Law § 530.12; Family Court Act § 842). It may mean you and the protected person have reconciled, share children, or need to live together again, and it can still lead to arrest for criminal contempt if you have any contact, even invited contact, before a judge signs a new order. Forbes Stranger represents spouses, partners, parents, and family members seeking to lift, modify, or vacate orders of protection in criminal court, Family Court, and Supreme Court in every borough. When you need your life back, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Violating an Order of Protection: Criminal Contempt 2nd Degree (PL § 215.50)

Criminal contempt in the second degree (Penal Law § 215.50) is a New York crime that accuses someone of intentionally disobeying a court order, and in domestic cases it is charged when someone is accused of contacting, visiting, or going near a person protected by an order of protection. It may mean New York prosecutors believe a text, call, social media message, or visit to the home violated the order, even if the protected person invited it, and it can carry up to 364 days in jail, a new order of protection, and a record that can turn the next alleged violation into a felony. Forbes Stranger defends spouses, partners, parents, and family members in criminal contempt prosecutions in every borough’s criminal court. When one message or visit becomes a new criminal case, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Criminal Contempt 1st Degree (PL § 215.51)

Criminal contempt in the first degree (Penal Law § 215.51) is a New York felony that accuses someone of committing one of the specifically defined acts in § 215.51, including qualifying threats, physical conduct, property damage, or repeat violations. Unwanted contact alone does not necessarily constitute this offense. It may mean New York prosecutors believe an encounter with a protected spouse, partner, or family member went beyond contact to threats or physical conduct, and it can carry up to four years in state prison, a felony record, and an order of protection lasting up to eight years after the sentence. Forbes Stranger defends spouses, partners, parents, and family members in criminal contempt in the first degree prosecutions in every borough. When an alleged order of protection violation is charged as a felony, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Aggravated Criminal Contempt (PL § 215.52)

Aggravated criminal contempt (Penal Law § 215.52) is New York’s most serious order of protection charge, and it accuses someone of causing physical injury to a protected person in violation of an order of protection, or of committing first-degree criminal contempt after a prior felony contempt conviction within five years. It may mean New York prosecutors believe a violation of the order involved an injury or followed an earlier felony contempt case, and it can carry up to seven years in state prison, a felony record, and an order of protection lasting up to eight years after the sentence. Forbes Stranger defends spouses, partners, parents, and family members in aggravated criminal contempt prosecutions in every borough. When prosecutors are seeking years in prison for an order of protection violation, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Served With a Family Court Order of Protection (Family Court Act Article 8)

A Family Court order of protection is issued in a family offense proceeding (Family Court Act Article 8), a civil case filed by a spouse, partner, ex, or relative who claims you committed harassment, assault, menacing, stalking, or another family offense, and the first temporary order is often signed without you present. It may mean you are being ordered out of your home or away from your children based on a petition you have not yet answered, and it can lead to a final order lasting up to two years, or up to five years with aggravating circumstances (Family Court Act § 842), findings that shape custody and visitation, and criminal contempt charges if the order is violated. Forbes Stranger defends spouses, partners, parents, and family members in Family Court family offense proceedings in every borough. When a Family Court petition threatens your home and your children, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and your accuser. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Surrendering Guns Under an Order of Protection (CPL § 530.14; FCA § 842-a)

When a criminal court or Family Court issues an order of protection, the judge can suspend your gun license and order you to surrender every firearm you own, and must do so in many cases involving alleged threats, weapons, or injury (Criminal Procedure Law § 530.14; Family Court Act § 842-a). It may mean you have to turn over your handguns, rifles, and shotguns within hours or days, and it can lead to the permanent loss of your gun license and a federal felony charge if you keep a gun while subject to certain orders of protection (18 U.S.C. § 922(g)(8)). Forbes Stranger defends licensed gun owners, hunters, security professionals, and retired officers facing firearm surrender orders in criminal court, Family Court, and before the NYPD License Division. When an order of protection puts your guns and your license on the line, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Domestic Violence Court in NYC, Including IDV Court (22 NYCRR Part 141)

Domestic violence courts are specialized parts in each borough’s Criminal Court and Supreme Court that hear only domestic violence cases. Integrated Domestic Violence (IDV) courts (22 NYCRR Part 141) go further, putting a family’s criminal case, Family Court matters, and divorce before a single judge. It may mean your case is in front of a judge who handles only domestic violence matters and a prosecutor from a specialized domestic violence bureau, and it can lead to frequent court dates, mandated batterer’s intervention programs, compliance monitoring, and, in IDV court, decisions on your criminal case, custody, visitation, and divorce made by the same judge. Forbes Stranger defends spouses, partners, parents, and family members in domestic violence and IDV courts in Manhattan, Brooklyn, Queens, the Bronx, and Staten Island. When one judge decides your freedom and your family’s future, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Domestic Violence Charges for Non-Citizens and Green Card Holders (8 U.S.C. § 1227(a)(2)(E))

For non-citizens, including green card holders, visa holders, and DACA recipients, a domestic violence conviction can trigger deportation under federal immigration law (8 U.S.C. § 1227(a)(2)(E)), and a court finding that you violated an order of protection can make you deportable even without a criminal conviction. It may mean that a plea offered as a quick resolution, even to a misdemeanor, could lead to removal proceedings, a denied citizenship application, or being barred from returning after travel abroad, and it can put your status at risk long after the criminal case is over. Forbes Stranger defends green card holders, visa holders, DACA recipients, and undocumented individuals in domestic violence prosecutions in every borough. When a domestic violence charge puts your future in this country on the line, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.

ACS Investigation After a Domestic Violence Arrest (Family Court Act Article 10)

An ACS investigation after a domestic violence arrest is a child welfare inquiry by New York City’s Administration for Children’s Services, usually opened when police report that children were present during a domestic incident, and it can lead to a neglect case in Family Court (Family Court Act Article 10). It may mean caseworkers will visit your home, interview your children, and ask you to sign a safety plan, and it can lead to court-ordered supervision, removal of your children, and an indicated report on the State Central Register that can block jobs working with children for years. Forbes Stranger represents parents and caregivers in ACS investigations and Family Court neglect proceedings in every borough. When ACS is at your door after an arrest, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and ACS. Call Forbes Stranger now.

Licensed Professionals Charged With Domestic Violence (Education Law §§ 6509, 6530)

For doctors, nurses, teachers, lawyers, pharmacists, accountants, real estate brokers, financial professionals, and other licensed professionals, a domestic violence charge can trigger reporting obligations to employers and licensing boards, and a conviction can be treated as professional misconduct (Education Law §§ 6509, 6530). It may mean your licensing board, school district, hospital, employer, or firm will learn of the arrest before your case is resolved, and it can lead to suspension, an investigation of your license, loss of your job, and public discipline that follows you long after the criminal case ends. Forbes Stranger defends licensed professionals in domestic violence prosecutions in every borough. When a domestic violence charge puts your career on the line, Forbes Stranger is the firm you want standing between you and the prosecution. Call Forbes Stranger now.
