Trial & Appellate Advocacy

Where the courtroom decides.

Trial advocacy for matters that will be decided in a courtroom — and appellate work grounded in the record below.

The firm prepares every case as though it will be tried. That is not a slogan about willingness; it is a description of how the work is organised. Cases built for trial negotiate differently, because the other side can see what the record will look like in front of a jury.

Trial practice

Cases are tried by the named partners. That includes jury selection, motions in limine, the examination of witnesses, and the closing argument — the parts of the case that cannot be delegated to a posture. Clients facing homicide, robbery, assault, sex offenses, and weapons charges are represented through verdict where trial is the right course.

Post-conviction and appeal

Where the record requires it, the firm pursues post-conviction motions and direct appeals in state and federal appellate courts. Appellate work is written with the trial record in view: the issues that matter on appeal are usually preserved long before the notice of appeal is filed, and the brief is built on what the record actually shows.

Preparing the record

Because the same firm handles the trial and any subsequent challenge, the record is made deliberately. Objections are preserved, hearings are requested where the facts support them, and the transcript is treated as the foundation of everything that follows.

Where trial is avoided

Trial is not always the right answer. Where a matter can be resolved through motion practice, negotiation, or a diversion appropriate to the facts, the firm pursues that path with the same preparation behind it. The point of readiness is the leverage it creates, not the trial itself.

This page describes the firm’s practice and is not legal advice. The facts of every matter differ; no outcome is promised or implied.
